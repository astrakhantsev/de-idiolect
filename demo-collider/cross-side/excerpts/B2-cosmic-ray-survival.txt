[Giddings & Mangano 2008 (arXiv:0806.3381), §2.2 "Cosmic ray collisions on Earth" — bounds side. Verbatim usage excerpt; inline formulae rendered to ASCII, cuts marked [...].]

Cosmic rays hit Earth with energies known to exceed 10^20 eV, corresponding to center-of-mass (CM) energies exceeding 100 TeV. Thus, anything that can be made with Earth-based accelerators up to this energy is already being made by nature. This argument can be used to effectively rule out the existence of particles predicted by some speculative scenarios, or to exclude possible macroscopic consequences of high energy collisions, as discussed for example in the literature. This argument requires however more attention if the large momentum imparted to the produced particles by the cosmic-ray kinematics has an impact on how they evolve after production.
