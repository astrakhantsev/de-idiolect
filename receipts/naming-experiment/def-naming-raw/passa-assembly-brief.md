A proposed automatic system, described operationally:

Groups of people who work together on a problem develop their own local ways of talking — they make up new words, and they use ordinary words with special local meanings. Other groups working on the same underlying things use different local words for them. As a result, when one group searches for what is already known, using its own words, it fails to find the other groups' work.

The proposed system has three parts:

1. It watches a group's writing and notices, automatically, when a word is being used in a group-specific way — both brand-new made-up words and ordinary words carrying a special local meaning.
2. At that moment it automatically writes a careful, self-contained explanation of what the word means there: what kind of thing it names, what its inputs and outputs are, what it asserts or does, when it applies. The explanation is written only in plain, common words drawn from a fixed small list, plus simple mathematical notation — no names of people, methods, or subjects of study are allowed inside it.
3. It stores these explanations and uses them — rather than the words themselves — to find places where OTHER groups write about the same thing under other names. Candidate matches are checked one pair at a time and labeled: same thing · more general · more specific · related.

A design commitment: explanations are produced one at a time, on demand, separately for each group. No shared master list of things-and-their-names is ever built, agreed on, or maintained across groups.
