### CONCEPT-1: the threshold-crossing value of the evidence multiplier

**Canonical terms.** I know of no established name for λ* itself. It is a composite of three canonical things. (a) The λ in `logit(p̂) = logit(π₀) + λ·D̄` is the **extremization coefficient** (also **recalibration parameter**) in **logit aggregation / logarithmic opinion pooling** of probability forecasts. (b) The numerator `logit(t) − logit(π₀)` is the **required weight of evidence** in the Turing/Good sense (weight of evidence = log Bayes factor; the "how many decibans to reach a verdict" question). (c) The move of fixing a posterior threshold and solving backwards for what would be needed is **reverse-Bayes analysis** (Good's "device of imaginary results"), and a parameter value at which a decision flips is called a **switching value** or **threshold value** in decision/sensitivity analysis. The ratio structure — distance to a boundary divided by mean evidence increment — is exactly Wald's average-sample-number logic in sequential analysis, which is presumably why the definition insists "not a count of anything." The same flip-point family contains Rosenthal's fail-safe N and the epidemiologists' E-value.

**Fields.** Statistical forecasting / judgment aggregation, Bayesian decision theory, sequential analysis; legal and forensic probabilism for the numerator (required posterior for a standard of proof).

**Oldest treatments.** Wald (1945), "Sequential Tests of Statistical Hypotheses," *Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, and Wald (1947), *Sequential Analysis*, Wiley. Good (1950), *Probability and the Weighing of Evidence*, Griffin; Good (1979) on Turing's decibans, *Biometrika* 66. Kaplan (1968), "Decision Theory and the Factfinding Process," *Stanford Law Review* (details from memory — verify). Pauker & Kassirer (1980), "The Threshold Approach to Clinical Decision Making," *NEJM*, for decision thresholds on probabilities. For the λ parameter specifically: Ranjan & Gneiting (2010), *JRSS-B*; Baron, Mellers, Tetlock, Ungar (2014), "Two Reasons to Make Aggregated Probability Forecasts More Extreme," *Decision Analysis*; Satopää et al. (2014), "Combining Multiple Probability Predictions Using a Simple Logit Model," *International Journal of Forecasting*; Genest & Zidek (1986), *Statistical Science*, for pooling generally.

**Confidence.** High on the component identifications; moderately high that λ* has no standard name. Verify first: the reverse-Bayes line (Matthews ~2001; Held, Matthews and coauthors, ~2019–2022 — I am unsure of exact titles/venues) for whether anyone has named an evidence-scaling flip point, and Satopää et al. 2014 for whether a "critical" extremization value appears.

### CONCEPT-2: accuracy requirement for a tuning-parameter estimator to beat a fixed default

**Canonical terms.** The specification *type* — "how accurate must a measurement be, given its intended use, before it is worth using" — is **target measurement uncertainty** in metrology (VIM3, JCGM 200) and **fitness for purpose** / **analytical performance specifications** in analytical and clinical chemistry. The *content* — a per-case estimated parameter losing to a single fixed value once estimation error passes a breakpoint — is the **forecast combination puzzle** (estimated combination weights vs. equal weights), the **1/N result** in portfolio choice, and the **unit-weights / improper linear models** literature in psychology. Measuring output as fraction of achievable Brier-score improvement is a **skill-score retention** analysis (Murphy's decomposition tradition). Establishing the schedule by injecting synthetic errors is standard **Monte Carlo sensitivity / noise-injection robustness analysis**. The specific schedule (RMSE of log λ̂ ≲ 0.3 → ~85%, ≈ 0.6 → ~50%, ≳ 1.0 → harmful) is almost certainly bespoke to your project; I would not expect to find those numbers anywhere.

**Fields.** Metrology and laboratory medicine (the spec type); econometric forecasting; judgment and decision making; ML hyperparameter/plug-in estimation.

**Oldest treatments.** Bates & Granger (1969), "The Combination of Forecasts," *Operational Research Quarterly*; Wainer (1976), "Estimating Coefficients in Linear Models: It Don't Make No Nevermind," *Psychological Bulletin* (title from memory); Dawes (1979), "The Robust Beauty of Improper Linear Models," *American Psychologist*; Cotlove, Harris & Williams (1970), *Clinical Chemistry* (the classic analytic-vs-biological-variation performance criterion — details hedged); Thompson & Fearn (1996), "What Exactly Is Fitness for Purpose in Analytical Measurement?", *Analyst*; Smith & Wallis (2009), "A Simple Explanation of the Forecast Combination Puzzle," *Oxford Bulletin of Economics and Statistics*; DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009), *Review of Financial Studies*; Brier (1950), *Monthly Weather Review*; Murphy (1973), *Journal of Applied Meteorology*.

**Confidence.** High on the family; near-certain the exact schedule is unpublished. Verify first: whether any forecast-combination paper parameterizes estimator quality as RMSE in log space and reports a retention curve — Smith & Wallis 2009 and the Claeskens et al. (~2016, *IJF*) treatment of the puzzle are where I would look.

### CONCEPT-3: blaming the filter, not the generator

**Canonical terms.** The two-stage structure is **classification with a reject option** (Chow) / **selective prediction**. The claim itself — the correct material is in the candidate set, the shortfall is in selection — is the **oracle gap** (n-best **oracle error rate** vs. actual, standard in speech recognition) and **reranking headroom** in NLP. In psychology the same decomposition is Koriat & Goldsmith's **monitoring-and-control** framework for memory reporting (retrieval generates; monitoring/control decides to volunteer or withhold), and more abstractly the signal-detection separation of **sensitivity vs. criterion** — "the deficit is criterion, not d′." In the recent LLM literature the relatives are the **generation–discrimination gap** (West et al., "The Generative AI Paradox," ~2023, venue hedged) and "models know more than they show" / calibration-of-abstention work (e.g., Kadavath et al. 2022, "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know," arXiv). Andrew Ng's informal "**ceiling analysis**" (~2011 ML course, not a formal publication) names the general practice of attributing pipeline shortfall to a stage.

**Fields.** Pattern recognition / ML (reject option, selective classification), speech and language processing (n-best oracle analysis, reranking), cognitive psychology and psychophysics (SDT, metacognition), and annotation methodology for the ambiguity framing.

**Oldest treatments.** Chow (1957), *IRE Transactions on Electronic Computers* (optimum character recognition with a reject rule) and Chow (1970), "On Optimum Recognition Error and Reject Tradeoff," *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*; Green & Swets (1966), *Signal Detection Theory and Psychophysics*, Wiley; Nelson & Narens (1990), *Psychology of Learning and Motivation*; Koriat & Goldsmith (1996), "Monitoring and Control Processes in the Strategic Regulation of Memory Accuracy," *Psychological Review* 103; N-best oracle analyses from ASR circa 1989–1990 (Chow/Schwartz and colleagues at BBN — exact citation hedged); Collins (2000), discriminative reranking, ICML; El-Yaniv & Wiener (2010), selective classification, *JMLR*.

**Confidence.** High — this concept has multiple genuine homes. Verify first: Koriat & Goldsmith 1996 (I'm confident of it) as your load-bearing pre-2015 anchor, and the exact venue of Chow 1957.

### CONCEPT-4: pooling everything raters put forward, then selecting by a fixed rule

**Canonical terms.** The elicitation-plus-aggregation shape is **approval voting** from social choice — each rater endorses a *set* of candidates rather than one — used here epistemically (truth-tracking). The engineering form is **candidate-set / n-best system combination**: **ROVER** and **confusion-network consensus** in speech recognition, and in the multiple-classifier-systems literature, combining classifiers at the **candidate-set (class-set) level by union**, which is known to beat combining top-1 decisions by voting — including the specific phenomenon that plurality over committed answers can fall below the best member. Related paradigms: **overgenerate-and-rank** in NLG/parsing; eliciting multiple answers from one rater in JDM (**"the crowd within,"** dialectical bootstrapping); and in annotation science, harvesting multiple defensible labels per ambiguous item (**CrowdTruth**, learning-from-disagreement / perspectivist NLP). The precise contract (one line per reading, paraphrase, no padding, optionally no final pick) is protocol-level detail I'd expect to be yours.

**Fields.** Social choice / epistemic democracy; speech and language processing; multiple classifier systems (pattern recognition); crowdsourcing and corpus annotation; judgment and decision making.

**Oldest treatments.** Brams & Fishburn (1978), "Approval Voting," *American Political Science Review* 72; Ho, Hull & Srihari (1994), "Decision Combination in Multiple Classifier Systems," *IEEE TPAMI* (explicitly treats unions/intersections of candidate class sets — verify that detail); Fiscus (1997), ROVER, IEEE ASRU Workshop; Kittler et al. (1998), "On Combining Classifiers," *TPAMI*; Mangu, Brill & Stolcke (2000), *Computer Speech and Language*; List & Goodin (2001), "Epistemic Democracy," *Journal of Political Philosophy*; Vul & Pashler (2008) and Herzog & Hertwig (2009), both *Psychological Science*; Poesio & Artstein (~2005) on annotating ambiguity with multiple readings (workshop paper, details hedged); Aroyo & Welty, CrowdTruth (~2013–2015; the *AI Magazine* piece is 2015).

**Confidence.** High on the family and on approval voting as the cleanest canonical anchor; moderate on Ho et al. 1994 containing the union-of-candidate-sets treatment specifically — that is the first thing I would verify, along with whether the epistemic-social-choice literature has a named result on approval beating plurality at truth-tracking (I believe such results exist but cannot cite one confidently).
