# Verification pass: Does TERMINOLOGY SCIENCE already own the proposed system?

**System components under test:** (a) detect coinage / project-specific word-sense; (b) auto-generate an operational definition in a *constrained common vocabulary* at coin time — lazily, per project, no global terminology resource ever built; (c) use the *definition* (not the term) as the key for cross-community concept matching with typed relations. Plus the overall **lazy-local, no-global-resource** stance.

**Bottom line up front:** Terminology science **clearly owns the raw ingredients but not the assembled machine.** It owns (a) coinage detection (neonymy + neology observatories + term extraction), the *structural rules* for writing definitions inside (b) (ISO 704's intensional/genus-differentiae/substitution/systemic definition; Faber's per-category definitional templates), and the *concept-oriented matching with typed relations* half of (c) (onomasiology + closed relation inventories). It does **not** own: the **constrained common defining vocabulary** in (b) — that is **lexicography's** invention (Ogden's Basic English 1930; the Longman Defining Vocabulary), and terminology definitions are explicitly required to be *system-specific*, the opposite of a shared common vocabulary; the **automatic, at-coin-time, lazy** generation in (b) — a 2025 survey finds LLM auto-definition still fails the field's own standards; the **definition-as-the-key** mechanism in (c) — terminology matches on the *concept* (concept-system position), not on a generated definition string; and the **no-global-resource** stance — mainstream terminology *builds* concept systems/termbases; only *ad hoc terminography* endorses per-project/no-global-termbase work, and it does so for human translators, term-first, with reuse.

## Q1 — "Ad hoc" vs "systematic" terminography (Cabré; Wright & Budin)

**Verdict:** Owns the **lazy-local stance** (per-project, on-demand, single-term, no systematic global termbase) — but as a *human translator workflow*, not automated, and not "definition as cross-community key." Does **not** own (b) automation or (c). One recent source *does* now connect ad hoc terminology work to LLMs, but only for equivalent-finding, not for the proposed pipeline.

**Owner + citation:** The ad hoc / systematic distinction is standard terminology-science doctrine, codified in the Wright & Budin *Handbook of Terminology Management* (1997) and Cabré's *Terminology: Theory, Methods and Applications* (1999); the "punctual"/ad hoc label is associated with Cabré. Contemporary statement: Faber, "Terminology" (2019, teaching chapter, lexicon.ugr.es), citing Lusicky & Wissik 2015.

**Body quotes:**
- Faber (2019), p. 2: *"Broadly speaking, there are two types of TOT work: (1) ad hoc terminology work and (2) pro-active terminology work (Lusicky and Wissik 2015). Ad hoc terminology work is performed case by case and involves collecting, understanding and processing a limited number of terms extracted from the source text during the translation process."*
- Wikipedia, "Terminology" (bodytext): *"Ad hoc work on terminology, which deals with a single term or a limited number of terms"* vs *"Systematic collection of terminology, which deals with all the terms in a specific subject field or domain of activity, often by creating a structured ontology of the terms within that domain and their interrelationships."* And: *"Ad hoc terminology is prevalent in the translation profession, where a translation for a specific term (or group of terms) is required quickly to solve a particular translation problem."*
- The LLM connection — "Next-gen terminology: Transforming terminology work with large language models," *Across Languages and Cultures* 26(S), 2025 (akjournals), §1: *"terminology research can also be done on an ad hoc basis to quickly resolve specific terminological issues. This approach is common among translators who need to find immediate equivalents for terms in the text they are working on (Bowker, 2020)."*

**Key nuance against ownership of the "no resource" reading:** ad hoc terminography in the literature is *term-first* and *reused*, not "no global resource ever built." A WebSearch excerpt (Bowker & Marshman, best-practices paper) states a translator's personal termbase stores *"terms that posed a challenge during a given translation project…"* and that integrated termbases *"[enable] reuse of terminology across translation projects."* So ad hoc ≠ ephemeral/discard; it is deferred, small-scale documentation that still accumulates into a (personal) resource.

**Confidence:** High that ad hoc terminography owns per-project/on-demand/no-systematic-global-termbase. High that it does **not** own automation-at-coin-time or definition-as-matching-key. Medium on the exact Wright/Cabré page wording (I quoted Faber/Wikipedia's renderings, not the Handbook page itself — see below).

**Could not verify:** The verbatim defining passage inside Wright & Budin's *Handbook* (1997) or Cabré (1999) — both are paywalled/preview-only; I relied on Faber's and Wikipedia's paraphrases plus the 2025 journal article. I could not find any pre-2020 source connecting ad hoc terminography to *automation of definition generation* specifically; the 2025 article connects ad hoc work to LLMs only for *equivalent finding*.

## Q2 — ISO 704 / ISO 1087: definition-writing rules; is there a constrained defining vocabulary?

**Verdict:** ISO 704 **owns the definition *structure*** that (b) would emit — intensional definition, genus + differentiae, substitution principle, systemic coherence — but prescribes **NO constrained defining vocabulary (no fixed word list).** Its rules are structural/validity/style rules. The "constrained common vocabulary" idea in (b) is **lexicography's**, not terminology's. Moreover ISO 704 demands definitions be *system-specific*, which is in tension with a shared "common" vocabulary.

**Owner + citation:** ISO 704:2009 / ISO 704:2022, *Terminology work — Principles and methods* (definitions in Clause 6, terms via ISO 1087:2019). First edition ISO 704:1987.

**Body quotes (from the full ISO 704:2009 text and the 2022 preview):**
- Definition = genus + differentiae. ISO 704:2022, entry 3.3: *"intensional definition: definition that conveys the intension of a concept by stating the immediate superordinate concept and the delimiting characteristic(s)."*
- ISO 704:2009 §6.2: *"Intensional definitions shall include the superordinate concept immediately above, followed by the delimiting characteristic(s). The superordinate concept situates the concept in its proper context in the concept system…"*
- **Substitution principle.** §6.3.4: *"The substitution principle shall be used to test the validity of a definition. In the case of an intensional definition, it is valid if it can replace a designation in discourse without loss of or change in meaning."*
- **Systemic (system-specific), not common.** §6.3.2: *"A definition shall reflect the concept system describing the concept and its relations to other concepts in the concept system. Definitions shall be systemic in order to enable a terminologist to reconstruct the concept system."*
- **Audience awareness — but style, not a word list.** §6.1: *"…it is important to take into consideration the needs of the intended audience: a) specialists in the subject field…; b) specialists in another subject field…; or, c) non-specialists…. A definition alone may not be sufficient."* This is as close as ISO 704 gets to "constrain the vocabulary," and it stops at *supplement for non-specialists*, never a fixed defining lexicon.
- **No circularity — the nearest thing to a vocabulary constraint.** §6.5.2: *"…the resulting definitions are said to be circular. Circular definitions make it impossible to fully understand the concept and shall be avoided…. The substitution principle clearly reveals repetition and circularity."*

**The constrained defining vocabulary belongs to LEXICOGRAPHY, not terminology (decisive for (b)):** A WebSearch synthesis (Longman/Ogden sources): *"The Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English (LDOCE) utilizes the Longman Defining Vocabulary, a 2200-word controlled defining vocabulary used to write all of the definitions in the dictionary… developed from Michael West's General Service List,"* and *"Basic English is a simplified controlled form… restricting its vocabulary to 850 carefully selected words"* (Ogden, 1930). Notably, the same source says the controlled defining vocabulary *"has also facilitated the use of the LDOCE as a machine-readable dictionary in computational linguistics research"* — i.e., the fixed-word-list-for-definitions idea already exists AND was already tied to machine-readability, but in **lexicography of learner's dictionaries**, not terminology science.

**Confidence:** High. I read ISO 704:2009's entire Clause 6 (nature, intensional, definition-writing, substitution, deficient/circular) plus the 2022 term entries. There is no controlled/constrained defining word list anywhere in it. High confidence the constrained-common-vocabulary concept is lexicography's.

**Could not verify:** The exact wording of ISO 1087:2019's entries for "definition"/"intension" (I saw them only as cited *sources* inside ISO 704). ISO 704:2022's Clause 6 body (pp. 33–42) is behind the iTeh preview cut-off; I quoted the identical substantive rules from the 2009 full text instead (the substitution principle and intensional-definition rules are materially unchanged).

## Q3 — Neonymy / neology observatories (Rondeau; Cabré's OBNEO): does detection wire to definition-writing?

**Verdict:** Owns **(a) institutionalized coinage detection** strongly — but detection is wired to **human lexicographic documentation**, not to an **automatic definition-generation next step**. So it predates/owns (a); it does **not** own (b)'s automatic definition step.

**Owner + citation:** Rondeau (1981/1984) coined *néonymie* (terminological neology). Cabré's Observatori de Neologia (OBNEO), Institut Universitari de Lingüística Aplicada, Universitat Pompeu Fabra (consolidated group since 1996); public outreach via the *Neolosfera* blog.

**Body quotes / evidence (OBNEO page is Cloudflare-blocked; quotes are from WebSearch renderings of upf.edu/obneo and Neolosfera coverage):**
- Rondeau distinction (WebSearch, néonymie sources): *"Guy Rondeau [1984] distinguished between 'neology' for general language and 'neonymy' as specific to terminology."* And *"Rondeau (1981) named néonymie d'origine and néonymie d'appoint… classifying specialized neologisms into two large groups: those appearing in languages together with knowledge production, and those appearing in processes of knowledge transfer between different speakers' communities."* (Note: that last split — coinage vs. cross-community transfer — is conceptually adjacent to your cross-community angle, though Rondeau does not operationalize it as definition-matching.)
- OBNEO detection method (WebSearch rendering of upf.edu/obneo/metodologia): *"Emptying a text consists of detecting and extracting lexical units considered to be neological according to lexicographical criteria, considering neological those words that are not recorded identically (in form, meaning and syntactic functioning) in the lexicographical exclusion corpus…"* — detection is defined as **absence from an exclusion dictionary**, which is precisely the "is this a coinage / new sense?" test in (a).
- The definition step is **separate, human, and downstream** (WebSearch rendering, Neolosfera): *"Neolosfera is a blog where a lexicographic neologism is published every day with information including: the first time it was registered by the Observatori, the type of neologism, two examples of its usage and an explanation about its origin, process of formation and meaning."* This is a curated *explanation*, published manually, not an auto-generated operational definition fired at coin time. A separate strand ("Dictionarization of Catalan Neologisms," Peter Lang) treats the *manual* passage from detected neologism to dictionary entry.

**Confidence:** High that OBNEO/neonymy own institutionalized detection. High that the detection→definition link is **manual lexicographic curation, not automation**. Medium on OBNEO wording (page blocked by Cloudflare; I quoted the search engine's rendering of the official page, corroborated across two OBNEO pages).

**Could not verify:** Whether any OBNEO-adjacent tool (e.g., their extraction pipeline) auto-drafts definitions internally — I found no evidence it does, and could not open the primary page directly.

## Q4 — Temmerman's sociocognitive terminology: per-community, non-universal definitions?

**Verdict:** **Owns the "non-universal, per-context/per-user, variable" definition stance** — the strongest terminology-science predecessor to the *idea* that definitions are local rather than global. But it argues this as a *descriptive theory of understanding* (prototype/templates), not as an *operational cross-community matching key*. Supports the lazy-local philosophy; does not own (b) automation or (c) definition-as-key.

**Owner + citation:** Rita Temmerman, *Towards New Ways of Terminology Description: The Sociocognitive Approach* (John Benjamins, 2000). Quotes below are from an open-access review of that book in *Lexikos* (journal), which quotes Temmerman's own propositions with page numbers.

**Body quotes (from the Lexikos review PDF; page numbers are the reviewer's citations to Temmerman):**
- Definitions arise locally from discourse, not stipulated globally: *"…definitions of the entity intron, the activity blotting and the collective category biotechnology do not originate as stipulations from outside for isolated concepts, but rather develop from descriptions in the environment of a specialist text. As units of understanding they have a prototype structure, may be subject to variation…"*
- New methodology name — templates, per category: the review's Chapter 3 heading, *"From Traditional Definitions of Concepts to Templates of Units of Understanding,"* and Temmerman (p. 73), quoted: *"Categories are all the units of understanding which are impossible to describe according to the principles of traditional Terminology. There is prototype structure, both within and between categories…"*
- Anti-univocity (anti-Wüster): Temmerman (p. 132), quoted: *"Univocity as the ideal for unambiguous and therefore efficient reasoning and communication is unrealistic for prototypically structured categories… polysemy is functional in specialised language."*
- The publisher/abstract statement of the same book (WebSearch rendering, benjamins.com) makes the per-community point explicit: sociocognitive principles include *"a combined semasiological and onomasiological perspective… form and content of definitions vary according to category types and user's requirements…"* — "vary according to… user's requirements" is the closest published wording to *per-community, non-universal definitions*.

**Confidence:** High that Temmerman owns the *non-universal / variable / user-dependent definition* thesis and the critique of Wüster's prescriptivism. High that she does **not** propose it as an automated cross-community matching mechanism.

**Could not verify:** Temmerman's *own* full-sentence wording beyond the review's quoted excerpts (the book is paywalled; I quoted the reviewer's direct quotations of it, with the reviewer's page cites).

## Q5 — Onomasiology and concept-oriented terminography (concept/definition → terms)

**Verdict:** Terminography **is by definition onomasiological** — searching *from concept to the terms expressing it, across languages/communities* — so it owns the **direction** in (c) and the **typed-relation** apparatus. But the matching key is the **concept** (its position in a concept system), *not a generated definition string*, and this is normally realized by **building a shared concept system** — the opposite of "no global resource."

**Owner + citation:** Classical terminology theory (Wüster; ISO 704 concept relations); Faber's Frame-Based Terminology (definitional templates + closed relation inventory); general onomasiology/terminography literature.

**Body quotes:**
- Faber (2019): semasiological vs onomasiological framing — *"a translator's personal term base is generally organized semasiologically (term-based) instead of onomasiologically (concept-based), though sometimes, it can be a mixture of the two (Bowker 2015)."*
- Faber (2019), on FBT and **typed relations** (directly relevant to (c)): *"Frame-based Terminology envisages the configuration of specialized domains on the basis of definitional templates and creates situated representations for specialized knowledge concepts. These networks are based on an underlying domain event as well as a **closed inventory of both hierarchical and non-hierarchical semantic relations.**"*
- Faber (2019), definitions as reusable mini-representations with templates (closest to (b)'s "operational definition"): *"Definitions can be regarded as mini-knowledge representations, which require a definitional frame or template for each category (Faber and Tercedor-Sánchez 2001…)."* And on *per-context* variation: *"FBT advocates the creation of flexible definitions in order to better account for multidimensionality… A single definition is not sufficient to describe multidimensional concepts that participate in various conceptual frames."*
- The onomasiological direction itself (WebSearch synthesis): *"Onomasiology starts with a concept and seeks the various terms used to express it across different languages and eras. Terminography takes an onomasiological approach, whereas lexicography takes a semasiological approach,"* and *"the notion of concepts is central, while terms are a sort of 'consequence'… term denotes only one concept. Concepts can be placed in a concept system that describes how concepts are related to each other."*

**Where (c) is NOT owned:** The cross-community match in standard terminography is anchored to the **concept** (concept-system node / concept identity), curated by humans, and typically materialized as a **shared/global concept system or termbase** — contrary to your "definition-is-the-key" and "no global resource" design. Corroborating the field's *system-specific* (not common/shared) definition requirement, the 2025 LLM survey warns auto-generated definitions fail precisely because they *"are not domain-specific and system-specific"* — i.e., the field wants definitions bound to a *local* system, not phrased in a *common* cross-community vocabulary.

**Confidence:** High that onomasiology + typed-relation inventories are owned by terminology science and predate the proposal by decades. High that the *definition-as-key* operationalization is **not** standard practice (concept-as-key is).

**Could not verify:** Whether any niche onomasiological system uses a *generated definition string* (rather than concept ID/notation) as the literal match key — I found none; the ONOMTERM onomastic-terminology database surfaced but I did not open it.

## Cross-cutting finding on component (b)'s automation (important)

The field has **already tried** LLM auto-definition and, as of 2025, judged it inadequate by its own standards — which both confirms the idea is *in the air* and confirms terminology science does **not** yet "own" a working version:

- "Next-gen terminology" (2025), §2.6 "Preparing definitions": *"…using LLMs to generate or extract definitions from multiple websites does currently not yield promising results, either for terminology (Heinisch, 2025) or for lexicography (Schryver, 2023), since the definitions do not adhere to the standard of definitions or are not domain-specific and system-specific."*
- Same article, on the **pieces** existing separately: *"From term extraction to definition generation and concept system creation, LLMs can be integrated into traditional workflows."* The tasks exist individually; the *lazy, coin-time, definition-as-cross-community-key* assembly is not described.
