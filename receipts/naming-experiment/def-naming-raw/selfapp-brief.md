THE EXPLANATION:

This names a proposed automatic helper for people who search for what is already known. It applies when groups that work apart develop their own local ways of talking — new made-up words, and ordinary words carrying special local meanings — so that one group's search, run in its own words, misses other groups' work on the same underlying things. The thing named is a process with three steps, run one item at a time, only when needed, separately for each group: (1) it watches a group's writing and notices when a word is being used in a group-local way; (2) at that moment it writes a short, self-contained explanation of what the word means there — what kind of thing it names, what goes in and what comes out, what it asserts or does, when it applies — using only plain, common words drawn from a fixed small list plus simple mathematical notation, never any names of people, methods, or subjects of study; (3) it uses that explanation, not the word, to find places where other groups write about the same thing under other names, checks candidate matches one pair at a time, and labels each: same thing · more general · more specific · related. It never builds or maintains a shared master list of things-and-their-names across groups, and anything it does store can be thrown away and re-made from the writings it came from. Its purpose is to raise the share of relevant, already-existing work that a group's search actually finds, without asking any group to change its words or to agree with any other group.
