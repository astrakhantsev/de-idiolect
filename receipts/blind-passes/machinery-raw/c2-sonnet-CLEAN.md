## (A) FROM KNOWLEDGE, BEFORE SEARCHING

- The vocabulary problem — information science/HCI — Furnas, Landauer, Gomez & Dumais, 1987, *CACM* — names and measures the core failure mode: people rarely pick the same word for the same thing — RECALLED
- MeSH controlled vocabulary — biomedicine/library science — U.S. National Library of Medicine, ongoing since ~1960 — human-assigned concept terms so retrieval doesn't depend on an author's literal wording — RECALLED
- ANSI/NISO Z39.19 and ISO 25964 thesaurus-construction standards — information science — formal rules for building synonym-mapping controlled vocabularies — RECALLED
- IPC/CPC patent classification — patent law, WIPO/USPTO/EPO — concept-based categories searched jointly with keywords specifically because keyword-only search misses synonymous prior art — RECALLED
- Citation chasing/snowballing — evidence synthesis/bibliometrics — long-standing manual technique formalized in the Cochrane Handbook — RECALLED
- Cochrane Handbook search-methods chapter — evidence-based medicine — mandates multi-database, multi-vocabulary, citation-chasing "highly sensitive" strategies — RECALLED
- Bates's "berrypicking," 1989 — information science — iterative, evolving queries instead of one static query — RECALLED
- Latent Semantic Analysis/Indexing — Deerwester, Dumais, Furnas, Landauer, Harshman, 1990 — same group's direct technical fix: SVD-based latent space so different wording for the same concept still matches — RECALLED
- Dense/neural retrieval (DPR, SBERT, ColBERT) — IR/NLP — embedding-based search built so machine searchers aren't limited to literal token overlap — RECALLED
- BEIR benchmark — Thakur et al., ~2021 — standardized multi-dataset benchmark measuring whether dense retrievers generalize across wording shifts — RECALLED
- SPECTER — Cohan et al., 2020, Allen Institute for AI — citation-graph-trained paper embeddings built for related-work/prior-art discovery — RECALLED
- CLEF-IP / NTCIR patent retrieval tracks, ~2009–2013 — evaluation campaigns for automated prior-art search — RECALLED
- TAR / TREC Legal Track, Grossman & Cormack ~2011 — measured recall of automated review vs. manual human review in e-discovery — RECALLED, numbers to verify
- Chemical structure search (CAS Registry/SciFinder, Markush search) — chemistry/patent law — indexes molecular structure, not text — RECALLED
- Co-citation (Small, 1973) / bibliographic coupling (Kessler, 1963) — bibliometrics — relatedness by shared citations, not shared words — RECALLED
- Clemen & Winkler, 1985 — decision analysis — formalizes how correlated sources reduce the value of "agreement" — RECALLED
- Pennell & Reichler, "On the Effective Number of Climate Models," 2011 — climate science — dozens of CMIP models share code lineage; effective independent count is much smaller than the nominal count — a strong non-obvious analogue of failure mode 2 — RECALLED
- Intelligence tradecraft: single-source reporting miscounted as multi-source corroboration (the "Curveball" case) — RECALLED, needs sourcing
- Search-engine result-overlap studies — Spink et al., ~2006 — measured actual overlap between engines' results — RECALLED
- Kish's design effect — survey statistics, 1965 — the mathematical tool for effective sample size under correlated observations — RECALLED
- Kleinberg & Raghavan, "Algorithmic Monoculture and Social Welfare," 2021, PNAS — models welfare loss when many decision-makers share one algorithm and make correlated errors — RECALLED
- Capture-recapture applied to systematic-review completeness — ecology-derived method estimating how much a literature search missed — RECALLED, authors unconfirmed

## (B) AFTER VERIFICATION

- **The vocabulary problem** — HCI/information retrieval — Furnas, Landauer, Gomez & Dumais, *Communications of the ACM* 30(11), 1987, pp. 964–971 — "In every case two people favored the same term with probability <0.20... the popular approach in which access is via one designer's favorite single word will result in 80-90 percent failure rates" — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/32206.32212 — SNIPPET (abstract text, confirmed by two independent bibliographic sources incl. a scholar's citation notes; full body paywalled behind ACM/Cloudflare, could not retrieve)
- **MeSH controlled vocabulary, official history** — NLM, first list 1954, thoroughly revised and renamed MeSH with the 1960 *Index Medicus (New Series)* — "MeSH provides a consistent way to find content with different terminology but the same concepts... The adoption of a single subject authority list... has two major virtues: simplicity for users... and economy to the Library" (Frank B. - **Combined classification + text search as the norm in patent examination** — USPTO — Manual of Patent Examining Procedure §904 — "Automated search tools covering patent documents usually provide both a classified and text search capability... it is rare that a text search alone will constitute a thorough search of patent documents. - **Correlated sources destroy the value of "agreement"** — decision analysis — Clemen & Winkler, "Limits for the Precision and Value of Information from Dependent Sources," *Operations Research* 33(2), 1985, pp. 427–442 (note: **journal conflict** — this paper is sometimes miscited as *Management Science*; confirmed here as *Operations Research*) — "positive dependence among information sources can have a serious detrimental effect on the precision and value of the information... the limiting value of information as more sources are considered can be considerably less than the expected value of perfect information" — https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.33.2.427 — SECONDARY (search-engine synthesis of abstract; not independently opened)

- **Algorithmic monoculture: shared algorithms produce correlated, not independent, decisions** — algorithmic economics/CS — Kleinberg & Raghavan, *PNAS* 118(22), 2021, arXiv:2101.05853 — "monocultural convergence on a single algorithm by a group of decision-making agents, even when the algorithm is more accurate for any one agent in isolation, can reduce the overall quality of the decisions being made by the full collection of agents. - **Kish's design effect: the general-purpose math for "how much independence is lost"** — survey statistics — Leslie Kish, *Survey Sampling*, 1965 — the design effect is the ratio of an estimator's actual variance under a correlated/clustered design to its variance under simple random sampling of the same size; effective sample size = nominal size ÷ design effect — confirmed via multiple independent methodological summaries (e.g., PMC10426793, Wikipedia "Design effect") — RECALLED/SECONDARY, book itself not opened (pre-digital text, "could not retrieve" a full-text source)
- **The reference interview: a human negotiation step with no real machine equivalent** — library/information science — Robert S. Taylor, "Question-Negotiation and Information Seeking in Libraries," *College & Research Libraries* 29(3), 1968, pp. 178–194 — Taylor's model distinguishes four levels of a searcher's own need (visceral, conscious, formalized, compromised) and has the librarian actively negotiate the query *before* searching, rather than taking the first wording at face value — https://crl.acrl.org/index.php/crl/article/view/16421 — SECONDARY/SNIPPET (search-engine synthesis; not independently opened)
- **Citation/"pearl-growing" and snowballing as the human technique underlying modern citation-search standards** — library science — long-standing practice, formalized name traced through LIS literature (e.g., PMC1175807, "Site-ation pearl growing: methods and librarianship history and theory") — "one citation is mined, by digging deeper into the reference list and then digging even deeper into the references cited by those references" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1175807/ — SECONDARY/SNIPPET (search-engine synthesis; not independently opened)

---

- **Oldest machinery found:** the 1960 U.S. National Library of Medicine MeSH thesaurus (itself built on a 1954 predecessor list) — a controlled-vocabulary fix for wording mismatch that predates computers doing the searching at all. If you count non-digital antecedents more loosely, faceted/analytico-synthetic library classification (Ranganathan's Colon Classification, 1933) is older still, but I did not verify it this session — flagging it as RECALLED, not confirmed.
- **Single most on-target thing built specifically for machine searchers:** citation-graph-trained dense embeddings for literature/prior-art discovery (SPECTER and its successors) — purpose-built so a machine comparing an idea's *wording* against a corpus is instead comparing its position in a learned semantic/citation space, which is the only entry in this list that (a) targets machine searchers specifically, not adapted from human practice, and (b) has a dedicated benchmark (SciDocs/BEIR) built alongside it to check whether it actually works.
- **The gap:** a single, named, cross-domain discipline that treats "several independent-seeming searchers converging on the same wrong answer" as its own measured problem, with an agreed metric for how much independence was actually lost — not found in these searches. What exists is scattered across climate science (effective number of models), ensemble ML (diversity measures, of contested validity), decision analysis (dependent-source value-of-information), survey statistics (design effect), and intelligence tradecraft (sourcing corroboration) — each with its own vocabulary, none citing the others, and no evidence in these searches of a body of work that unifies them or that measures, specifically, how much independence *multiple AI/LLM search agents* lose when they derive queries from the same input wording.
