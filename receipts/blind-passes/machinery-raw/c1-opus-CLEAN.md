- **The independence assumption, destroyed experimentally** — software reliability engineering — John Knight & Nancy Leveson, *IEEE TSE* SE-12(1):96–109, Jan 1986. From §5, "Model of Independence": *"There were twenty seven versions (i.e. N = 27), one million tests were executed (i.e. n = 1,000,000), and the number of tests in which more than one version failed was 1255 (i.e. K = 1255). - **The most on-target machinery for machine searchers: statistical certification of "nothing more is there"** — IR / e-discovery — David D. Lewis, Eugene Yang & Ophir Frieder, *CIKM '21*, DOI 10.1145/3459637.3482415, arXiv:2108.12746. From the body: *"Most stopping rules for one-phase TAR workflows lack valid statistical guarantees"* and, damningly, *"with one narrow exception, previously proposed certification rules fail to meet their purported statistical guarantees."* They ship two rules — the Quantile Point Estimate Threshold (QPET) and Quantile Binomial Confidence Bound (QBCB). The crucial requirement is stated plainly: *"Certification rules, on the other hand, use a random sample to provide a formal statistical guarantee that the stopping point has [been reached]."* That is the price of admission: a labeled random sample drawn from a **bounded, enumerable corpus**. It does not port to an open-web searcher, because you cannot random-sample "the published literature." — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.12746 — **PRIMARY-BODY**
- **CAPA — the agreement problem, measured in machines** — ML — Shashwat Goel, Joschka Strüber, Ilze Amanda Auzina, Karuna K Chandra, Ponnurangam Kumaraguru, Douwe Kiela, Ameya Prabhu, Matthias Bethge, Jonas Geiping (Tübingen/ELLIS/Stanford), arXiv:2502.04313, ICML 2025. From §5.2: *"It could undermine benefits from using LM juries by compromising independence and amplifying collective biases. Most concerningly, our results indicate that as model blind-spots get harder to detect, making us defer more to AI oversight, models also make more similar mistakes, posing safety risks from correlated failures."* Judge affinity bias is measured: *"We find a significant (p<0.01) positive correlation (average Pearson r=0.84) between LLM-as-a-judge scores and model similarity."* Setup: 130 models from OpenLLM Leaderboard 2, MMLU-Pro and BBH, similarity computed only against **models from different developers** to exclude distillation as a confounder. — https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04313v2 — **PRIMARY-BODY**

- **Agreement raising confidence without raising accuracy — measured in humans** — social psychology / complex systems — Jan Lorenz, Heiko Rauhut, Frank Schweitzer & Dirk Helbing (ETH Zürich), *PNAS* 108(22):9020–9025, 16 May 2011. - **Citation indexing — the oldest wording-free machinery, verified lineage** — law → bibliometrics — Shepard's Citations, Frank Shepard, **1873**; adapted to science by Eugene Garfield, *"Citation Indexes for Science; A New Dimension in Documentation through Association of Ideas," Science* 122:108–111, **1955** (Science Citation Index launched 1964). Three closing lines:

1. **Oldest machinery found:** Shepard's Citations, 1873 — a legal citator indexing on the citation relation rather than on wording, verified only at SECONDARY level via the Garfield lineage. Older candidates (Roget 1852, Cutter's syndetic "see" references 1876) are RECALLED and unverified; if the question is what I *opened*, the oldest is Blair & Maron 1985.
2. **Single most on-target thing built specifically for machine searchers:** Lewis, Yang & Frieder's quantile-estimation certification rules (QPET/QBCB, CIKM 2021) — the only machinery I verified that attaches a valid statistical guarantee to the decision to stop searching, and it is honest that nearly every prior rule *"fail[s] to meet their purported statistical guarantees."* Its precondition — a labeled random sample from a bounded corpus — is also the clearest statement of why the guarantee cannot follow a machine onto the open literature.
3. **The gap:** I looked for and did **not find in these searches** — (a) any benchmark that scores a machine on correctly saying *"not present"*, i.e. with vocabulary-shifted positives planted and true negatives included; every novelty/search benchmark I opened (CiteME, CLEF-IP) guarantees an answer exists, so a false-novelty rate is measured nowhere I reached; (b) any port of PRESS-style independent search-strategy peer review, relative recall, or capture–recapture to LLM search agents; (c) any metric of independence between *search agents sharing a query* — CAPA and the Cornell correlated-errors work both require per-item ground truth on multiple-choice benchmarks, and Goel et al. state outright that free-text similarity metrics do not yet exist, which is precisely what measuring lost independence between two novelty verdicts would require. Not found in these searches — not "does not exist."
