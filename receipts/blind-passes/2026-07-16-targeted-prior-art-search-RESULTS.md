# Targeted prior-art search — results

## How to read the labels (revised after review)

**Evidence level** — the prior single `VERIFIED` flag conflated four different things, so it is decomposed here. Where a bullet below still carries a bare `VERIFIED`, read it as the level named in its parenthetical.
- `primary-full` — the whole source was read.
- `primary-partial` — the relevant section was read (e.g. Carpineto §1), not the whole.
- `primary-abstract` — only the publisher's own abstract was read; the body is unread.
- `secondary-only` — a secondary source accurately carrying a quotation; the primary was never opened.
- `snippet-only` — search-result snippet.
- `id-confirmed` — bibliographic identity agrees across databases. **Says nothing about the substantive claim.**

**Novelty status attaches to a CLAIM, not to a source.** A source can foreclose one claim and leave its neighbour wide open. Statuses below are scoped to the exact claim named in the bullet. Two standing rules from the review, both accepted: *(a)* **never assign `foreclosed` from `snippet-only` or `secondary-only` evidence**; *(b)* `foreclosed` means "a theorem or definition makes this terminal," never "a big neighbouring field looks close."

**Three claim types are distinguished** (they were blurred in the first draft): **direct prior art** (someone did this exact thing), **methodological antecedent** (mature machinery exists that a solution would use), and **analogy** (a different domain's structure resembles this one; transfer requires its own argument).

**Absence claims here mean "not identified among the sources listed in this file"** — never "does not exist," and not even "not found in the searches," because **the searches themselves are not recorded**: no frozen query list, no result or screening counts, no stop rule. This file records *hits and venues*. Named unsearched literatures are listed at each null. **The §3b null shows the stakes: it was written by an agent that never read the paper refuting half of it.**

### 3e — The patent leg (run 2026-07-16, after the §3b null was flagged as unsafe)

> **Why this domain:** patent search is the only place where finding-all-prior-art is a **professional, adversarial, legally-consequential** task with money on it, where applicants **coin their own vocabulary by legal right** ("applicant as own lexicographer," MPEP 2173.05(a), `primary-full`), and where the misses get **denominated after the fact** by someone paying to find them. ### 3c — The countermeasure field (ontology alignment / schema matching / entity resolution)

> **Scope correction (review finding 2, accepted).** The first draft treated this field as *foreclosing* the idiolect trap. It does not. Matching entities between **two known, bounded, already-identified ontologies** is a different task from **detecting, under open-world retrieval, that a project's coined vocabulary denotes an already-published finding somewhere in the literature**. The benchmark scores below are mature methods on bounded datasets with a supplied reference alignment — they do not show the general problem is solved, because the general problem includes *finding the other ontology in the first place*, which is the whole difficulty here.
