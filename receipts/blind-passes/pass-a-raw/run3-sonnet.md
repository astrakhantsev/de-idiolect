## (A) From knowledge, before searching

- Undiscovered public knowledge — library & information science — Swanson, D.R. (1986), "Undiscovered Public Knowledge," *Library Quarterly* 56(2) — claim: two complementary bodies of literature can be logically connected (A affects B; B affects C; therefore A may affect C) without either ever citing the other, so the connection sits unmade though every piece is public — RECALLED
- The vocabulary problem — HCI / information retrieval — Furnas, Landauer, Gomez, Dumais (1987), "The Vocabulary Problem in Human-System Communication," *CACM* — claim: two people spontaneously pick the same keyword for a common object far less than half the time, so keyword search fails even when the target content exists under a different word — RECALLED
- Literature-based discovery — biomedical informatics — Swanson & Smalheiser (1990s), building on Swanson 1986 — a field built specifically to exploit vocabulary-disconnected literatures algorithmically — RECALLED
- Jangle fallacy (sibling: jingle fallacy) — psychology / psychometrics — jingle: Thorndike (1904); jangle formalized: Kelley (1927), *Interpretation of Educational Measurements* — jangle: assuming two differently-named things are different constructs when they are the same one — RECALLED
- Multiple independent discovery — history/sociology of science — Ogburn & Thomas (1922), "Are Inventions Inevitable?," *Political Science Quarterly*; Merton (1961), "Singletons and Multiples in Scientific Discovery" — catalogs cases of the same discovery made independently by separated groups — flagged even in recall as ADJACENT, not identical (simultaneity, not delayed rediscovery) — RECALLED
- Incommensurability — philosophy of science — Kuhn (1962), *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* — paradigms carry mutually untranslatable vocabularies, so practitioners can't recognize they're discussing the same problem — ADJACENT (stronger, more totalizing claim) — RECALLED
- Surface vs. structural similarity in analogical transfer — cognitive psychology — Gick & Holyoak (1980), "Analogical Problem Solving," *Cognitive Psychology* — people index remembered problems by surface/vocabulary features, not deep structure, so a structurally identical prior solution isn't spontaneously retrieved without a hint — RECALLED
- Obliteration by incorporation — sociology of science — Merton (concept; exact date not recalled) — an idea's source stops being cited because it's been absorbed into general knowledge — ADJACENT (forgetting a *known* origin, not failing to find an unknown one) — RECALLED
- Taxonomic synonymy / "the synonym problem" — biological taxonomy — no single canonical paper recalled; addressed institutionally via nomenclatural codes (ICZN/ICN) and the rule of priority — RECALLED

The oldest thing found: Thorndike (1904), *An Introduction to the Theory of Mental and Social Measurements/Measures*, p.14 — naming the "jingle" fallacy (attributed there to a "Professor Aikins"), 23 years before Kelley's 1927 "jangle" complement and decades before any of the information-science or cognitive-psychology treatments.

If forced to pick one true home: library & information science, via Swanson's "undiscovered public knowledge" — it is the only literature that names the phenomenon *as a search failure over a fixed, findable public record*, rather than as a fact about constructs (psychometrics), paradigms (philosophy of science), memory retrieval (cognitive psychology), or simultaneous invention (history of science) — the others describe adjacent mechanisms or adjacent failure modes, not this one directly.
