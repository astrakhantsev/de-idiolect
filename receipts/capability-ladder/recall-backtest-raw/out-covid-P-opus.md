## Identification

What you've described is the **droplet-versus-airborne (droplet-versus-aerosol) dichotomy** of respiratory-infection transmission, and specifically the discredited **"5-micron threshold"** that supposedly separates the two routes. In the recent literature that dismantled it, this is named explicitly as the **"5-µm myth"** (also "5-micron dogma" / the "artificial" or "false dichotomy" of droplets vs. aerosols). The underlying physics you're gesturing at — how far a droplet travels before it either falls or evaporates into a floating residue — is the **Wells evaporation–falling curve**, and the floating residues are **"droplet nuclei"** (a term Wells coined). The clinical operationalization (surgical mask + distance vs. N95 + negative pressure) is the CDC/WHO framework of **transmission-based precautions** ("droplet precautions" vs. "airborne precautions").

**Direct answers to the three embedded questions:**

- **How far do droplets travel / where does the line actually fall?** The conventional 1–2 m "droplet falls" figure and the 5 µm cutoff are *not* physically grounded. Wells' own physics puts the fall-vs-float crossover at roughly **~100 µm** (humidity-dependent, ~60 µm at 90% RH up to ~125 µm at 0% RH) — an error of ~20× in diameter and ~1000× in particle mass versus the 5 µm figure. And exhalations don't obey a clean 1–2 m limit: a cough/sneeze is a **warm, moist turbulent gas cloud (puff)** that carries droplets of all sizes and can reach **7–8 m**, with small droplets remaining suspended for minutes to hours and dispersing across a room. So "large droplet = ballistic, short-range, ≤2 m" vs. "small = airborne" is a continuum artificially cut in two, not a real physical boundary.

- **What established the 5 µm line?** Not a study of droplet ballistics at all. Historians of the field (Randall, Ewing, Marr, Jimenez, Bourouiba) trace it to a **conflation, over the 1940s–1960s TB literature, of two unrelated size facts**: (a) Wells' actual droplet-fate cutoff (~100 µm), and (b) the ~5 µm size below which particles penetrate deep into the **alveoli of the lung** (relevant to where an inhaled particle *deposits*, and to TB, whose infectious dose can ride on a droplet nucleus carrying a single bacillus). The ~5 µm *deposition/infectivity* number got mislabeled as the *droplet-vs-airborne* boundary and was then hard-coded into CDC and WHO isolation-precaution guidance for decades.
