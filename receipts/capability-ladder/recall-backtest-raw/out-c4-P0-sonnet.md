## Findings

**1. Standard name(s).** There is no single established term for the whole recipe — it's a composite of two separately-named ideas:
- The per-judge move ("enumerate every defensible reading instead of committing to one") = **human label variation** / **"crowd truth"** in annotation methodology.
- The aggregation move ("pool the enumerated sets via union, apply a fixed rule; beats tallying single committed votes") = **approval voting**, with the general "a panel can beat its best member" intuition rooted in the **Condorcet Jury Theorem**.

**2. Owning fields.** Approval voting and the jury theorem belong to **social choice theory / voting theory** (political science, economics, mathematics). Human label variation / crowd truth belongs to **computational linguistics / crowdsourcing / annotation methodology** (NLP, HCI). The "union of candidate sets beats any single member" idea also has a machine-learning analog in **multiple-classifier-systems / ensemble learning** ("oracle" combiners), though I could not pin that to one canonical originating citation with confidence.

**3. Oldest/canonical treatments:**
- Condorcet, N. de (1785). *Essai sur l'application de l'analyse à la probabilité des décisions rendues à la pluralité des voix.* Paris: Imprimerie Royale. — origin of the Condorcet Jury Theorem, the classical justification that a panel's aggregate judgment can exceed any individual member's. **Pre-2015, high confidence** on existence/attribution; I have not read the original French text myself, only secondary summaries.
- Brams, S. J., & Fishburn, P. C. (1978). "Approval Voting." *American Political Science Review*, 72(3), 831–847. — founding paper of approval voting (each voter/judge submits a *set* of acceptable options rather than one). **Pre-2015, high confidence**, verified via Cambridge Core listing.
- Aroyo, L., & Welty, C. (2013). "Crowd Truth: Harnessing Disagreement in Crowdsourcing a Relation Extraction Gold Standard." *ACM Web Science 2013 (WebSci'13)*, Paris. — earliest canonical "crowd truth" paper arguing for capturing all plausible annotator readings instead of forcing one gold label. **Pre-2015, high confidence.** Companion/synthesis piece: Aroyo, L., & Welty, C. (2015). "Truth Is a Lie: Crowd Truth and the Seven Myths of Human Annotation." *AI Magazine*, 36(1), 15–24 — more widely cited but is 2015, not pre-2015.
- Kuncheva, L. I. *Combining Pattern Classifiers: Methods and Algorithms* (Wiley, 1st ed. 2004) — standard reference covering the ensemble "oracle" combiner (credit the ensemble if *any* member's output is correct — the ML analog of taking the union). **Medium confidence only** — my search confirmed the book covers "oracle"/related ensemble-diversity material but I could not verify the exact page/section, and there may be an earlier, more specific originating paper (e.g., in the multiple-classifier-systems literature of the mid-1990s) that I did not track down.

**4. Confidence, explicitly hedged:**
- **High:** No single pre-existing name covers the complete described pipeline end-to-end; it's a recombination.
- **High:** Aroyo & Welty (2013) is the right pre-2015 anchor for "enumerate all defensible readings, don't force one gold label."
- **Medium-high:** Approval voting (Brams & Fishburn 1978) is the right social-choice anchor for "pool sets by union + fixed rule, beats single-vote tallying" — but classical approval-voting theory is framed around human elections and preference expression, not accuracy/truth-tracking claims. I could not find one single canonical paper that makes the specific epistemic claim ("set ballots can be more *accurate* than single-choice ballots") as cleanly as the project's note states it; that specific truth-tracking framing may be a more recent or less centralized sub-literature.
- **Medium:** The ML-ensemble "oracle"/coverage framing as a parallel established idea — real and well-known in that field, but I'm not confident I've named its single most-canonical or earliest source.
- **Low/uncertain:** Whether the project's specific combination (enumerate → dedupe → union → fixed pick rule, explicitly benchmarked against best-single-judge and plurality-vote baselines) has already been named as its own methodology somewhere in recent LLM-as-judge evaluation literature — my searches on that specific modern framing did not turn up a distinct coined term, only the general "label-only vs. rationale-aware aggregation" contrast in current LLM-panel papers, which is adjacent but not the same claim.
