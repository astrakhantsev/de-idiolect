## Identification

**1. Standard name(s):** **Extremizing** (also "extremization") of pooled/aggregated probabilistic forecasts via log‑odds (logit) scaling — mathematically equivalent to **Platt scaling** applied to the geometric mean of odds. In the notation of this literature: final log‑odds = baseline log‑odds ("starting credence") + *d* × (mean pooled log‑odds − baseline), where *d* is the **extremizing factor / Platt coefficient** — your "multiplier that scales the pooled evidence lean." *d* can be set to a fixed constant a priori (commonly √3 ≈ 1.73, or empirically 1.161–3.921) or estimated/learned per question or per dataset — exactly the fixed‑default‑vs‑tuned‑estimate framing in the prompt.

**2. Field(s):** Judgment and decision making / decision analysis (its founding home — Tetlock/Mellers/Baron's forecasting‑tournament research program), forecasting science (*International Journal of Forecasting*), with more recent formal treatments in algorithmic game theory / theoretical CS (aggregation of "substitutable signals") and applied ML‑based judgmental forecasting.

**3. Oldest and most canonical treatments:**
- Baron, J., Mellers, B. A., Tetlock, P. E., Stone, E., & Ungar, L. H. (2014). "Two Reasons to Make Aggregated Probability Forecasts More Extreme." *Decision Analysis*, 11(2), 133–145. doi:10.1287/deca.2014.0293 — the canonical psychological/statistical justification for extremizing.
- Satopää, V. A., Baron, J., Foster, D. P., Mellers, B. A., Tetlock, P. E., & Ungar, L. H. (2014). "Combining Multiple Probability Predictions Using a Simple Logit Model." *International Journal of Forecasting*, 30(2), 344–356. doi:10.1016/j.ijforecast.2013.09.009 — the founding empirical/methodological paper; derived the extremizing‑factor range [1.161, 3.921] from IARPA geopolitical forecasting‑tournament data. Both are pre‑2015, as requested.
- Deeper root (why plain averaging under‑disperses at all): Ranjan, R., & Gneiting, T. (2010). "Combining Probability Forecasts." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B*, 72(1), 71–91 — established that averaging calibrated forecasts yields an uncalibrated (under‑confident) aggregate, the theoretical motivation extremizing corrects for. (Medium confidence this is "the" oldest root — it predates the extremizing terminology itself.)
- Modern theory for choosing *d* automatically (closest match to your "fixed default vs. estimate" framing): Neyman, E., & Roughgarden, T. (2021). "Are You Smarter Than a Random Expert? The Robust Aggregation of Substitutable Signals." arXiv:2111.03153 (later published at ACM EC 2022).
- Closest match I found to your exact "starting credence + multiplier × pooled deviation, fixed vs. per‑question factor" phrasing: Sevilla, J. [Nuño Sempere forum]. "Principled Extremizing of Aggregated Forecasts." EA Forum, 2021.
- Most recent applied treatment, explicitly discussing fixed‑a‑priori vs. learned (in‑/out‑of‑distribution) scaling coefficients for an LLM forecasting system, and proving Platt scaling ≡ log‑odds extremization: Alur, Stadie, et al. (Bridgewater AIA Labs). "AIA Forecaster: Technical Report." arXiv:2511.07678 (Nov 2025).
