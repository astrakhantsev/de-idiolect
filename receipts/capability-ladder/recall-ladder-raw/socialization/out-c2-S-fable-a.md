## The decode (check this — my whole answer rides on it)

Written in log-odds, your audit unit computes `logit(final) = logit(start) + m · (pooled evidence)`, where `m` is a slope you either estimate per-case or fix at a default. That single equation is the shared object of all the literatures below. ## Standard names, fields, and canonical treatments

**1. The multiplier itself (the recalibration/shrinkage factor).**
- **Calibration slope / recalibration slope** — biostatistics & clinical prediction. Origin: **Cox, D.R. (1958), "The Regression Analysis of Binary Sequences," JRSS-B 20:215–242** — proposed regressing binary outcomes on predicted log-odds; slope < 1 = predictions too extreme (overconfident), > 1 = too timid. This is *the* canonical anchor. Confidence high; the *method* is Cox's, though the *term* "calibration slope" is later (secondary-verified, consistent across sources).
- **Heuristic / uniform shrinkage factor** — **Van Houwelingen, J.C. & le Cessie, S. (1990), "Predictive value of statistical models," Statistics in Medicine 9(11):1303–1325**. A single factor `S` multiplies the fitted coefficients (the "evidence") to fix overfitting. Confidence very high (exact cite corroborated across multiple sources). I verified from the Riley 2021 primary that the standard form is literally `ln(p̂/(1−p̂)) = α* + S·(β̂·X)` — your "multiplier scaling the pooled evidence lean," verbatim.
- **Platt scaling** (Platt 1999) and **temperature scaling** (Guo et al., ICML 2017) — the ML-calibration names for the same scalar-on-the-logits.
- General statistical root: **shrinkage estimation** — **Stein (1956); James & Stein (1961)**.

**2. The "operating requirement" (how good the estimate must be before it beats the default).**
- This is the **bias–variance / when-to-shrink** decision, and specifically the documented result that an *estimated* shrinkage factor can be **worse than not shrinking** — your "past ~1.0 the estimate is worse than the fixed default." In decision theory this failure mode is the **reverse Stein effect** (shrinking toward a data-chosen target forfeits the guarantee). In your exact domain it's **Van Calster et al. (2020), "Regression shrinkage methods for clinical prediction models do not guarantee improved performance," Stat. Methods Med. Res.**, and **Riley, Snell, Martin, Collins et al. (2021), "Penalization and shrinkage methods produced unreliable clinical prediction models especially when sample size was small," J. Clin. Epidemiol. 132:88–96** — which I read directly: "penalization methods are not a 'carte blanche' … they are more unreliable when needed most … the consequence is miscalibration." That is your operating requirement, already named. Confidence high (primary-verified).
- The "**noise manufactures overconfidence**" mechanism is the **reliability (calibration) term** in **Murphy, A.H. (1973), "A new vector partition of the probability score," J. Appl. Meteorol. 12:595–600** (Brier = reliability − resolution + uncertainty); propriety generalized by **Gneiting & Raftery (2007), JASA 102:359–378**. A noisy multiplier inflates sharpness without resolution, and a strictly proper score charges you for it. Confidence high.

**3. The "cold-start" twist (no resolved cases yet; the default is assumed, not fitted).**
- "**Cold-start**" is itself a borrowed term (recommender systems, Schein et al. 2002). Applied to calibration, the label-free version is **unsupervised / label-free calibration** — e.g., "Unsupervised Temperature Scaling" (2019) — and the sample-size-for-reliable-recalibration question is exactly what Riley et al. (2021) quantify. Your specific point (at cold-start the fallback is only *assumed*, so the bar the estimate must clear differs) is a reasonable framing I did **not** find stated verbatim, but it follows directly from that literature. Don't claim it as new; confidence moderate that it's unstated, low that it's conceptually novel.

**4. The forecast-aggregation neighbor — the one your word "pooled" points at most directly.**
If "pooled evidence lean" means aggregated forecasts, the multiplier is the **extremizing coefficient** on the pooled log-odds. Canonical: **Karmarkar (1978)** (one-parameter log-odds transform), **Satopää, Baron, Mellers, Tetlock, Ungar et al. (2014), "Combining multiple probability predictions using a simple logit model," Int. J. Forecasting 30:344–356**, and **Baron, Mellers, Tetlock, Stone, Ungar (2014), "Two reasons to make aggregated probability forecasts more extreme," Decision Analysis 11:133–145**. I read a primary (Han & Budescu 2022, JDM) that states the design choice in your exact terms: "one can apply a pre-determined value … or estimate the optimal parameter γ" — i.e., fixed default vs. per-case estimate — with γ > 1 producing extremizing (overconfidence) and γ < 1 de-extremizing. Confidence high (primary-verified).
