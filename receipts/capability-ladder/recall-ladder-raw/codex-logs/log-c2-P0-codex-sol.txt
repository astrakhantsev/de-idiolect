Handley, Paxson, and Kreibich use “cold-start problem” for a monitor that begins operating without the prior state needed to interpret already-running activity. Their description is unusually close to the project’s wording. [USENIX discussion](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/sec01/full_papers/handley/handley_html/node5.html)

   If the note instead concerns staffing and activating a human audit organization, the closest professional term is **initial operational capability (IOC)**: the point at which a unit is trained, equipped, supported, and able to perform its mission. [U.S. Department of Defense Inspector General, 1991](https://media.defense.gov/1991/Mar/18/2001714514/-1/-1/1/91-063.pdf)

   It is probably **not “audit readiness,”** which ordinarily means that the *auditee* has records and controls ready for examination, rather than that the audit unit can operate from a cold start.

2. **Fields:** The primary home is **computer security**, especially intrusion detection, event-stream monitoring, and stateful runtime monitoring. The organizational interpretation belongs to **systems engineering/defense acquisition** under IOC and to **internal-audit governance** under establishment and resourcing of an audit function.

This is the earliest direct, canonical treatment I located of the **cold-start problem for a monitor**: after startup or restart, missing historical state can make ongoing activity uninterpretable.    - U.S. Department of Defense Inspector General, 1991, *Use of the Baseline Concept in Managing Major Weapon Systems Acquisitions*, Report No. 91-063. It defines **initial operational capability** as first effective employment by an adequately trained, equipped, and supported unit. The book covers establishing an audit organization and is an early canonical professional treatment. [Bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Practice_of_Modern_Internal_Auditing.html?id=75Km0AEACAAJ)

Later standards explicitly require a charter, authority, independence, access, and adequate competencies—the prerequisites for an operational audit unit. [IIA archived standards](https://www.theiia.org/en/standards/what-are-the-standards/mandatory-guidance/standards/attribute-standards/)
