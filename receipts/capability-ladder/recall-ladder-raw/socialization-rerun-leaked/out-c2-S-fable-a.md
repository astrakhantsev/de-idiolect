# Verdict: the phenomenon is established prior art under several names; the specific quantified schedule appears to be unpublished, but I would not claim the *concept* as novel

Bottom line: every structural component of your "cold-start operating requirement for the audit unit" exists in the literature, in some cases for 40–50 years. Your **multiplier** is what forecast aggregation calls the **extremizing / recalibration parameter**; your **operating requirement** (tuned-per-case vs. fixed default under estimation error, with a breakeven where the estimate becomes worse than the default) is a known result-shape called **estimation risk** in finance and the **"flat maximum" / unit-weights result** in judgment psychology, with an exact quantified analogue in DeMiguel et al. 2009; your **noise-manufactures-overconfidence** mechanism is Erev–Wallsten–Budescu 1994; and **"cold start"** is itself a borrowed recommender-systems term (Schein et al. 2002). What I did **not** find anywhere is your specific artifact: an estimator-agnostic error-tolerance schedule keyed to RMSE of the log-multiplier (0.3 → ~85% of gain, 0.6 → ~half, ~1.0 → breakeven), derived by synthetic error injection, for the extremization parameter at cold start. So the honest write-up framing is "a quantification, for this parameter and regime, of a well-known tradeoff" — not "a new concept." Details and confidence levels below.

## 1. The multiplier itself: "extremizing" / "recalibration" — forecast aggregation & decision analysis (confidence: high, verified)

A multiplier scaling pooled log-odds evidence away from a prior is exactly the **extremization factor** in probability forecast aggregation:

- [Ranjan & Gneiting 2010](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/72/1/71/7076442), *JRSS-B* 72(1):71 — proves any nontrivial linear pool of calibrated forecasts is *necessarily* uncalibrated and requires recalibration (their beta-transformed pool). This is the canonical statistical justification for the multiplier's existence. Pre-2015 ✓.
- [Baron, Mellers, Tetlock & Ungar 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2015---two-reasons-to-make-aggregated-probability-forecasts_1.pdf), "Two Reasons to Make Aggregated Probability Forecasts More Extreme," *Decision Analysis* 11(2):133–145. Pre-2015 ✓.
- [Satopää, Baron, Foster, Mellers, Tetlock & Ungar 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260030454_Combining_multiple_probability_predictions_using_a_simple_logit_model), "Combining multiple probability predictions using a simple logit model," *IJF* 30(2):344–356 — literally a fitted systematic multiplier `a` on pooled log-odds; optimal values found in [1.16, 3.92] on geopolitical questions. Pre-2015 ✓.
- [Turner, Steyvers, Merkle, Budescu & Wallsten 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-013-5401-4), "Forecast aggregation via recalibration," *Machine Learning* 95:261–289 — fitting the recalibration on resolved cases, i.e. exactly the "fitted default" your cold-start regime lacks. Pre-2015 ✓.
- Oldest Bayesian root: [Bordley 1982](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.28.10.1137), "A Multiplicative Formula for Aggregating Probability Assessments," *Management Science* 28(10):1137–1148 — multiplicative/log-linear pooling, of which extremization (weights summing >1) is the natural special case.
- Modern canonical treatment of when the multiplier should be >1 vs <1: [Lichtendahl, Grushka-Cockayne, Jose & Winkler](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.2176), "Extremizing and Antiextremizing in Bayesian Ensembles of Binary-Event Forecasts," *Operations Research* 70(5):2998–3014 (2022; [arXiv 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.02391)).

(ML's Platt scaling (1999) and temperature scaling are the same multiplier under another name — stated from memory, not verified this session.)

## 2. The operating requirement: "estimation risk" / the flat-maximum result (confidence: high that this is prior art for the concept)

The question "how good must a fitted parameter be before it beats a dumb fixed default?" is owned by at least three fields, with quantified breakeven schedules pre-2015:

- **Judgment psychology / psychometrics** — the unit-weights literature: [Wainer 1976, "Estimating coefficients in linear models: It don't make no nevermind,"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit-weighted_regression) *Psychological Bulletin* 83(2):213–217, and [Dawes 1979, "The robust beauty of improper linear models in decision making,"](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/dawes/the-robust-beauty-of-improper-linear-models-in-decision-making.pdf) *American Psychologist* 34:571–582 (plus Einhorn & Hogarth 1975 on unit weighting — cited via secondary sources, not read directly). Core result: with noisy estimation, fitted weights lose to fixed equal weights; sometimes called the **"flat maximum effect."**
- **Finance** — the closest structural match I found anywhere: [DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal 2009](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/5/1915/1592901), "Optimal Versus Naive Diversification: How Inefficient is the 1/N Portfolio Strategy?", *Review of Financial Studies* 22(5):1915–1953. They compute exactly an operating-requirement schedule: the estimation window needed before the fitted optimizer beats the fixed 1/N default (~3000 months for 25 assets, ~6000 for 50). Same result-shape as yours, different parametrization (data quantity rather than parameter RMSE). Pre-2015 ✓.
- **Kelly betting** — the breakeven-and-asymmetry structure: [MacLean, Ziemba & Blazenko 1992 and MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba's later work](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf) on fractional Kelly. Overbetting 2× the optimum yields *zero* growth while underbetting 2× keeps ~75% — a direct analogue of your "past RMSE ~1.0 the estimate is worse than the default" breakeven, including the asymmetry (over-extremization hurts more than under-extremization, which is why symmetric noise in the log-multiplier nets out as harmful overconfidence). Statistics' shrinkage/James–Stein/empirical-Bayes tradition (James & Stein 1961; Efron & Morris 1970s) is the same principle in general form — stated from memory, not verified this session.

## 3. Noise manufactures overconfidence (confidence: high, verified)

[Erev, Wallsten & Budescu 1994](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2F0033-295X.101.3.519), "Simultaneous over- and underconfidence: The role of error in judgment processes," *Psychological Review* 101(3):519–527 — the canonical demonstration that random error in a judgment pipeline produces apparent overconfidence. Pre-2015 ✓. Your mechanism ("noise in the estimated multiplier manufactures overconfidence") is this phenomenon localized to one parameter.

## 4. Cold start (confidence: high, verified)

- The term is from recommender systems: [Schein, Popescul, Ungar & Pennock 2002](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/564376.564421), "Methods and Metrics for Cold-Start Recommendations," SIGIR 2002:253–260. Pre-2015 ✓. (Amusingly, Ungar co-authored both this and the extremization papers.)
- The substantive problem — what multiplier to use with *no resolved history* — has a published principled answer: Neyman & Roughgarden's a-priori extremization factor (→√3 for large pools), derived from assumptions rather than fitted history ([arXiv 2111.03153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03153); applied to Metaculus data in [this EA Forum analysis](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/biL94PKfeHmgHY6qe/principled-extremizing-of-aggregated-forecasts)). That is exactly an "assumed default at cold start." The same post explicitly flags that fitted extremizers like Satopää's risk overfitting — the community knows the qualitative problem.
- Weather-forecast postprocessing (EMOS) publishes minimum-training-data requirements for recalibration parameters (typically 20–40 days, more for longer leads — e.g. [this comparison paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3763)), which is the meteorologists' version of an operating requirement in data-quantity units.

## What survives as potentially yours (confidence: low-to-medium — hedged)

I found **no** publication that states an error-tolerance schedule *keyed to the RMSE of the log extremization parameter*, estimator-agnostic via synthetic error injection, in the no-resolution-history regime. That specific quantification may genuinely be unclaimed. But three cautions: (a) my sweep was ~10 searches over a few hours, and the adjacent literatures (empirical Bayes, ML calibration, EMOS postprocessing) are vast — I'd specifically check Satopää's later work (the partial-information framework and the 2021 BIN model) and the Neyman–Roughgarden robust-aggregation line before committing; (b) converting between "how much training data" (DeMiguel, EMOS — published) and "what parameter error" (yours) is fairly elementary, so a reviewer may read yours as a reparametrization of known results; (c) your own project history argues for caution — the dashboard records that the `m*` work was already ceded to Clemen & Winkler 1985, and the 07-16 sweep killed the judge-dependence novelty claim at a depth my search here has not reached. My recommendation: write it up as "we quantify, for the extremization parameter at cold start, a tradeoff long established in shrinkage/estimation-risk terms" and cite the lineage above — that framing is both safe and, per your 07-16 lesson, more credible than a bare novelty claim.
