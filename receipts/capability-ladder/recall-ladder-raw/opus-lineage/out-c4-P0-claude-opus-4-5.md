## Identification

The concept "read the enumerations, not the votes" — a claim about extracting and using the reasoning/rationale content from panel judges rather than merely aggregating their final verdicts — corresponds to several established concepts across multiple fields:

### 1. **Premise-Based vs. Conclusion-Based Aggregation** (Judgment Aggregation Theory)

**Standard names:** Premise-based procedure (PBW), Conclusion-based procedure (CBW), Discursive Dilemma, Doctrinal Paradox

**Fields:** Social choice theory, Legal theory, Philosophy (epistemology of groups), Computer science (multi-agent systems)

**The concept:** The premise-based procedure aggregates judgments on the *reasons/premises* and derives collective conclusions logically from those; the conclusion-based procedure aggregates only the final verdicts directly. "Read the enumerations, not the votes" advocates the premise-based approach.

**Canonical citations:**
- **Kornhauser, Lewis A. & Sager, Lawrence G. (1986).** "Unpacking the Court." *The Yale Law Journal*, 96: 82–117. **(Original identification of the doctrinal paradox)**
- **Kornhauser, Lewis A. & Sager, Lawrence G. (1993).** "The One and the Many: Adjudication in Collegial Courts." *California Law Review*, 81: 1–59.
- **List, Christian & Pettit, Philip (2002).** "Aggregating Sets of Judgments: An Impossibility Result." *Synthese*, 140(1-2): 207–235. **(Formalization via impossibility theorem)**
- **List, Christian (2006).** "The Discursive Dilemma and Public Reason." *Ethics*, 116(2): 362–402.

**Confidence:** HIGH — this is the most precise conceptual match. The vocabulary is exact: "enumerations" = premises/reasons; "votes" = conclusions. The 1986 Kornhauser & Sager paper in Yale Law Journal is the canonical origin.

### 2. **Argument Mining / Rationale Extraction** (Computational Linguistics / NLP)

**Standard names:** Argument mining, Rationale extraction, Annotator rationales

**Fields:** Natural Language Processing, Computational linguistics, Explainable AI

**The concept:** Extracting the structure of reasoning and argumentation from text rather than just the final classification/verdict. The field treats the reasoning trace as the primary object of interest, not just the output label.

**Canonical citations:**
- **Palau, Raquel Mochales & Moens, Marie-Francine (2009).** "Argumentation Mining: The Detection, Classification and Structure of Arguments in Text." *ICAIL '09*.
- **Walton, Douglas, Reed, Chris & Macagno, Fabrizio (2008).** *Argumentation Schemes*. Cambridge University Press. **(Foundation for computational argumentation)**
- **Lawrence, John & Reed, Chris (2019).** "Argument Mining: A Survey." *Computational Linguistics*, 45(4): 765–818. **(Definitive survey)**
- **Zaidan, Omar F., Eisner, Jason & Piatko, Christine (2007).** "Using 'Annotator Rationales' to Improve Machine Learning for Text Categorization." *NAACL-HLT 2007*. **(First use of annotator rationales in ML)**

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH — good conceptual match for extracting reasoning rather than aggregating verdicts, but the LLM-judge context is more recent (2023+).

### 3. **Deliberative vs. Aggregative Democracy** (Political Philosophy / Social Choice)

**Standard names:** Deliberative aggregation, Deliberation vs. aggregation, Information pooling

**Fields:** Political philosophy, Deliberative democracy, Social choice theory

**The concept:** Deliberative approaches extract and weigh reasons/arguments exchanged during discussion; aggregative approaches count votes without examining the rationale structure.

**Canonical citations:**
- **Dryzek, John S. & List, Christian (2003).** "Social Choice Theory and Deliberative Democracy: A Reconciliation." *British Journal of Political Science*, 33(1): 1–28.
- **Habermas, Jürgen (1996).** *Between Facts and Norms*. MIT Press.
- **Cohen, Joshua (1989).** "Deliberation and Democratic Legitimacy." In *The Good Polity*, ed. Hamlin & Pettit.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — the framing fits but is more about political legitimacy than technical evaluation methodology.

### 4. **Reasoning Tree Auditing / Rationale-Based Aggregation** (Multi-Agent LLM Systems)

**Standard names:** Reasoning tree auditing, Rationale-based aggregation, Critical divergence point analysis

**Fields:** Multi-agent LLM systems, AI evaluation

**The concept:** Recent work (2024–2026) explicitly contrasts majority voting (which "discards the evidential structure of reasoning traces") with methods that audit the reasoning trees to identify where agents' logic diverges.

**Key citation:**
- **Yang, Wei et al. (2026).** "Auditing Multi-Agent LLM Reasoning Trees Outperforms Majority Vote and LLM-as-Judge." *arXiv:2602.09341*. **(Introduces AgentAuditor; this paper explicitly makes your claim)**

**Confidence:** HIGH for conceptual match, but this is very recent work (not "established" in the pre-2015 sense).
