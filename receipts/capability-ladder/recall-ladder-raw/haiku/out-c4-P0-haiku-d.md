## 1. **Standard Names**

This concept is formally known as:
- **The doctrinal paradox** (in jurisprudence and legal theory)
- **The discursive dilemma** (in social/political philosophy)
- **Judgment aggregation** or **logical aggregation** (in formal decision theory)

The phrase "read the enumerations, not the votes" expresses the **premise-based approach** to aggregating panel judgments, contrasted with conclusion-based voting.

## 2. **Disciplines**

- Legal theory and jurisprudence
- Political philosophy and social epistemology  
- Formal logic and decision theory
- Economics (rational choice theory)

## 3. **Oldest and Most Canonical Treatments**

**Foundational sources:**

1. **Kornhauser, L.A. (1992).** "Modeling collegial courts. II. Legal doctrine." *Journal of Law, Economics & Organization* — introduces the doctrinal paradox in appellate court context. [395 citations]

2. **Kornhauser, L.A. & Sager, L.G. (1993).** "The one and the many: Adjudication in collegial courts." *California Law Review* — the major foundational statement; proposes the "metavote" solution for resolving the paradox. [644 citations]

3. **List, C. & Pettit, P. (2002).** "Aggregating sets of judgments: An impossibility result." *Economics & Philosophy* — generalizes the problem to voting contexts using the term "discursive dilemma"; develops formal impossibility results. [900 citations]

4. **Dietrich, F. & Mongin, P. (2010).** "The premiss-based approach to judgment aggregation." *Journal of Economic Theory* — formalizes the logic of premise-based vs. conclusion-based aggregation. [111 citations]

5. **Grossi, D. & Pigozzi, G. (2022).** *Judgment aggregation: a primer* — comprehensive contemporary overview of the field.
