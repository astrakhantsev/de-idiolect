# The idiolect trap in the wild: a day-by-day, self-inflicted case study

## What this document is

The claim this document supports: groups (down to a single investigator working with LLM agents) coin private vocabulary for objects that established fields already own, run their novelty searches *in that vocabulary*, receive confident nulls, and read "not findable from here" as "unoccupied." The evidence here is not a constructed example but a case where it happened on the record — it happened to my own project, three times at the level of a headline novelty claim, and roughly twenty times at the level of individual renamings (not all of one class — the headline section gives the breakdown), over eighteen days of documented work, numbered below as Day 1 through Day 18.

This is a reconstruction from the project's internal working documents (research logs, pre-registrations, review transcripts, a claims inventory), compiled by two file-reading agents and re-verified on the load-bearing rows. One verification statement covers the whole document: every owner mapping detailed here was verified against the primary text — the cited work was opened and its quoted content checked, either during the events or in the subsequent audit. Renamings whose owners were mapped only at abstract level, without that check, are counted in the headline's funnel but not detailed in this document; they remain flagged as asserted in the project's novelty ledger. Two limits on the verification, stated up front: it covers the *citation*, not the full operational equivalence of the mapping — rows carry claim-level caveats inline, and the atomic claim-by-claim mapping (each object's estimand, assumptions, relation type) is the job of the project's novelty ledger, not this narrative; and an external reader cannot audit the internal chronology. What is checkable from outside: the load-bearing owners are published, citable works, and bibliographic pinpoints beyond what this narrative states live in the linked novelty ledger and citation audit. The internal history is offered as a documented narrative; the checkable content is the citations.

## Background: what the research was trying to do

The setting is a personal research program on knowledge-refinement tooling: one investigator (background: PhD and publications in automatic term recognition, later machine learning) orchestrating LLM agents (Claude- and GPT-family), with adversarial cross-model review as standard practice. Two consecutive efforts matter here.

**Program A — the source-dependence detector (Days 1–15).** Research goal: when a verdict rests on many sources (news reports, studies, forecasters, LLM agents), the sources are usually correlated — copies, shared upstream origins, shared methods — so the *effective* number of independent looks is far smaller than the count. The program aimed to build a detector that estimates that effective number from the texts themselves and corrects overconfident aggregation. The central quantity was written `N_eff = N/(1+(N−1)ρ̄)`; the program coined terms like `m*` ("the audit unit" — how many independent looks a verdict needs) and "the operating requirement" (the accuracy an instrument must reach to be worth building).

**Program B — judge-dependence (Days 15–18).** Research goal: when LLM judges evaluate the same claims, their errors correlate; is that correlation fixed in the model weights, or can elicitation (prompting, aggregation design) reduce it? The program ran ~20 experimental legs and ~7,000 model calls, and coined its own vocabulary along the way: "gate," "credence," "coverage," "read the enumerations, not the votes," and its keystone finding, "the signal, not the cut, is the bottleneck."

Both programs practiced what most researchers would recognize as diligence: pre-registrations, adversarial review of every major digest, and repeated prior-art searches. That is what makes the case instructive — the trap fired *anyway*, because the searches were run in the programs' own coined vocabulary.

## The headline

The project's conceptual vocabulary across both programs — `effective-N`, `m*`, "operating requirement," "decision-sensitivity," "gate," "credence," "coverage," "signal-not-cut," "read the enumerations," "set contract" — turned out, term by term, to be local renamings of objects owned by design-of-experiments, decision theory, meta-analysis, forecasting, psychometrics, argumentation, and proper-scoring-rule theory: roughly twenty renamings at the vocabulary level. **The twenty are not one class**, and counting them as one would repeat the inflation this document warns against. A post-hoc funnel over the full internal ledger sorts them into **~3 genuine recall misses** (dated coinage, a novelty search that failed to find the owner, the owner verified later), **~10 knowing borrowings** (the field's term was cited at or near coinage — context, not misses), **~4 self-errors** (the project misusing or confounding its own quantities), and **~4 rows whose owners remain asserted or defective** — that last class is not detailed in this document; it stays flagged in the project's novelty ledger. Three renamings reached the level of a headline novelty claim that then died:

1. **`m*`** (the "audit unit") → **Clemen & Winkler 1985** (the equivalent-number-of-independent-sources result). *Verified against primary text (Day 15).* Precision matters here: the **Kish design effect** connection was *asserted internally from Day 3* — a knowing borrowing, not a miss. What the project's searches failed to surface for two weeks was the forecasting-aggregation literature that owns the question outright.
2. **"the operating requirement"** → **value-of-information / target-product-profile threshold analysis** (Raiffa & Schlaifer 1961 lineage). *Verified against primary text (Day 15).*
3. **"the signal, not the cut, is the bottleneck"** (Program B's keystone) → **Bröcker 2008**, a proper-score uniqueness theorem extending DeGroot & Fienberg 1982. *Verified clean against the primary PDF (Day 18)*: "the forecasting scheme π^γ achieves the best possible average score among all forecasts for which γ is sufficient," and under a strictly proper score it "is uniquely defined through this optimum property." **Foreclosed — with the narrow scope a theorem-kill requires.** The foreclosure attaches to the *theoretical* claim: no headroom above the calibrated forecast given the signal, under strictly proper scoring rules. The program's own empirical analyses ran on Youden's J, which is *not* strictly proper, so they sit **outside the theorem's scope** — recorded in the internal audit as a methodological defect (the headline metric is one the relevant theory does not cover), not as an escape from the foreclosure. A 2026 preprint recorded in the project's novelty ledger (a mapping asserted there, not itself primary-verified) contradicts the slogan's broadest reading outside symmetric costs — a further reason the *general* slogan, as coined, was never a defensible claim.

The project's own diagnosis, recorded identically at each instance: *our invented vocabulary hid the occupation. The lesson is not "search harder" — it is search in the field's vocabulary, not ours, and before the program, not after.* It ran that sweep too late three times running.

## Program A — event flow (Days 1–15)

- **Day 1** — Program scoped. A missed-terminology sweep is *declared owed* on day one; status recorded as "no prior art found, NOT confirmed absent." The idiolect risk is named before any experiment runs.
- **Day 3** — Design committed. The central rename is made explicit as an *assertion*: the project's `N_eff` formula **is** the Kish design effect. The pre-mortem's item #7 names the exact idiolect risk: a terminology sweep might find the core claim already exists, retracting novelty mid-build.
- **Days 4–5** — Pre-test round 2 (round 1 killed by adversarial review). Load-bearing negative: a cheap factor-salience baseline *beats* the expensive perturbation method.
- **Day 8** — The owed novelty sweep is run and returns "novelty survives" (a 5-cluster same-model fan-out plus a deep read of two 2026 near-neighbours). **This resolution is overturned a week later.**
- **Day 9** — The interventional method beats the cheap baseline on the pre-registered comparison; an independent audit verifies the numbers and raises five process findings.
- **Day 10** — Prior-art collisions land: the phenomenon under study already has three field names (circular reporting / non-independence / correlated error). A rule is adopted in writing: *"DON'T claim we invented debate for independence."*
- **Day 11** — Deepest sweep yet: `m*` maps onto the fail-safe-N / E-value / same-decision-probability family; the extremization line traces to Satopää and Karmarkar (catching a *withdrawn* preprint in the process); a label-leak in an evaluation set is caught by review.
- **Day 12** — A worked example on the dietary-cholesterol literature ("are eggs bad for me?"). The field already owns the evidence-overlap metric the example was coining: **corrected covered area (CCA)**, from the umbrella-review literature.
- **Day 13** — The detector thesis breaks: the frozen estimand *inverts as a magnitude* on a constructed case (a fully-copied 6-source pool scores m̂ = 4.47 against a truth of 1), and a tuned global constant absorbs the entire correction, undercutting the per-question detector.
- **Day 14** — The headline "78 forecasters ≈ 1 independent update" is **retracted as a self-confound**: `m` is an extremization coefficient, not an independence count — an independent-by-construction pool of 78 yields the same number. The project had committed the very confound it existed to name, in its own headline. Five kickoff premises are overturned against full text; two cited reviews turn out not to exist.
- **Day 15** — The program is dropped. The claims inventory retracts its own novelty ratings: `m*`'s aggregation-literature owner is Clemen & Winkler, newly verified (the Kish connection had been asserted since Day 3); operating requirement = value of information / TPP — both *verified directly*. A meta-document written specifically to eliminate quantity-role substitution errors is found to have committed that error at least four times, caught by adversarial review in ~15 minutes.

## Program B — event flow (Days 15–18)

- **Day 15** — New direction: is cross-judge LLM error correlation *elicitation-dependent* or *weights-locked* (the open lever in Kohli 2026)? The decision record's own worry list flags "over-eager pivoting" and notes the replacement direction "is genuinely more interesting… a suspicious feeling worth flagging as such."
- **Day 16** — Ambiguity screen; a dissociation surfaces (near-perfect reading-of-enumerations under ambiguity vs failed gating); a regime-shift anomaly is observed (a model's hedge gate halving under byte-identical prompts).
- **Day 17** — Re-anchor to the registered question. The dependence wall measures in (effective judge count 1.2–1.35); repeat- and elicitation-rungs buy nothing; an earlier "beats the best single judge" claim is retired as regime-specific.
- **Day 18** — Held-out evaluation: every constructive recipe fails its pre-registered transfer bar while the mechanism findings replicate. The keystone is formulated: "the signal, not the cut, is the bottleneck."
- **Day 18** — The prior-art sweep — triggered by the investigator's own doubt (*"I'd assume there is tons of literature… not clear we have any true edge"*) and designed per the Day-15 lesson: four agents over *disjoint* literatures plus an adversarial refuter. Verdict: **nothing survives at claim level.** The keystone is **foreclosed by Bröcker 2008** (verified against the primary text). The internal record names the moment explicitly as the third occurrence, citing the two prior ones. The program is closed.

## What the trap did and did not cost

A separation the chronology requires: **technical invalidation and novelty invalidation were distinct events.** Program A's detector broke on its own terms (the estimand inversion of Day 13, the self-confound of Day 14) *before* the Day-15 prior-art verification; Program B's constructive recipes failed their held-out transfer bar before the Day-18 sweep. So the eighteen days were not spent *because of* the idiolect trap, and this document does not claim they were. What the trap demonstrably added is the **novelty-claim lifecycle**: three claims survived as believed-novel for days to weeks past the point where the field's vocabulary would have surfaced their owners, each protected by a confident null from a search run in the project's own terms. What day-one field vocabulary would have *saved* — different scoping, earlier kills, redirected effort — is a counterfactual, and it is stated here as a hypothesis, not a measurement.

## Three structural lessons the record states about itself

1. **Recognized in advance, and it still bit.** The Program-A pre-mortem (Day 3) named the idiolect risk explicitly; a novelty sweep (Day 8) declared "novelty survives"; on Day 15 the two headline claims were verified predated and the program dropped. Foreknowledge of the failure mode did not prevent it — because the sweep was run *in the coined vocabulary, over overlapping literature*. The defense that works is structural (search in the field's vocabulary, before the program), not attentional.
2. **Agreement among same-era searchers was worth approximately nothing — on this record's two observed occasions.** Occasion one (Day 15, Program A): a "6/6 agents agree the claim survives" verdict was one search run six times; a three-agent fan-out across *disjoint* literatures plus a refuter overturned it in about five minutes. Occasion two (Day 18, Program B's sweep — four agents over disjoint literatures plus a refuter): the three agents searching post-2023 preprints converged on "the identifiability framing survives," and the refuter overturned the convergence by pointing into 1990s psychometrics (Rasch-model estimability). One detail that is itself lesson 3 in action: the refuter's *specific citation* was later found defective in the audit (real quotes, wrong venue, a truncation hiding a qualifying sentence) — so the record supports "the convergence was overturned by an older literature the agents never searched," not the stronger "a verified 1991 paper killed the claim." In both observed cases the lever was disjoint literatures plus an adversarial refuter, not more agreeing agents; I state that as this record's pattern, not a universal law.
3. **Within verification, the defects live in the provenance layer, not the arithmetic.** Across the audited kills, every checked quote was real — and the citations around them were defective at a high rate (a help-manual quote attributed to a journal paper; an omitted hedge; conflicting identifiers). Meanwhile none of the project's *computed* numbers was ever found wrong. Scope this correctly: the record also contains failures that are neither provenance nor arithmetic — estimand drift and quantity-role substitution, the self-error class in the ledger — so the full audit surface is three layers (provenance of sources, validity of the estimand, computation), of which computation was the only one that never failed here. The first layer is why every row in the ledger above required the primary text; the second is why the ledger separates `retracted (self-error)` from `predated`.

## Which of these events can serve as clean test cases for de-idiolection?

De-idiolection — generating a community-neutral definition of a coined term and searching with the definition rather than the term — is the countermeasure this case study motivates. It is tempting to reuse the events above as its test set. Applying the eligibility bar a fair test requires (documented real miss × not model-memorized × opaque *label* over a non-obvious concept × human-asserted ground truth):

| candidate | verified? | documented miss? | memorization screen | verdict as a test case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| operating requirement → value of information | yes | yes | survives (the bare coinage misroutes models into wrong fields; the neutral definition reaches the owner) | **the one clean case — and it has already been run** (definition-mediated naming experiment, Day 18) |
| `m*` → Clemen & Winkler | yes | yes | not tested | borderline: the label is opaque but the concept (effective sample size) is textbook — real risk it voids at the definition level |
| dietary-cholesterol overlap → CCA | yes | **no — CCA was cited in the same internal document that coined the local term** (verified in the subsequent audit) | n/a | **dropped — not a genuine miss**; usable only as a constructed routing demo |
| keystone → Bröcker 2008 | yes | yes | not tested; proper-scoring theory likely memorized | weak test, useful as *demonstration* — recovering it merely re-confirms the theorem that closed the program |
| readings-not-votes → Rowe & Wright | quotes yes, hedge omitted | yes | not tested | marginal; the citation needs its hedge restored before any use |
| the "78 forecasters ≈ 1" self-confound | yes | n/a (self-error) | n/a | not a recall case at all — but the clearest single illustration of the failure class |

**Net test yield: one clean recall case, already run.** In that experiment — per-case predictions were sealed before scoring, but the cross-case pattern was read after the fact, so this is a **pilot with a post-hoc reading, not a validated result** — the definition arm beat the term arm on exactly the one *opaque* coinage (operating requirement) and did nothing on the transparent slogan or the already-known-field case. I call the pattern the **transparency gradient** — de-idiolection earns its place on opaque coinages over non-obvious owners, not on transparent phrasings, and not where the field was already in hand at coinage — and grade it a **scope hypothesis** from a four-case pilot on the same history that motivates the countermeasure. Validation would need held-out cases selected before outcomes are known; none exist on this project's history, which is the point of this table.
