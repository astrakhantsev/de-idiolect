# Diary saved me from blaming the wrong config change

Spent Monday convinced our reread ration enforcement had silently broken, because three agents that used to pass the file-navigation suite started failing en masse. Went to the rig diary before touching anything and found the actual change two entries back: someone bumped the cap from four to two the same day, unrelated to what I was originally investigating. Cap of two is just too tight for a five-file task, not a bug in the enforcement code at all. Would've spent the whole day debugging the wrong thing without that log.

While in there I also noticed a groove lock case buried in the failure logs from the same batch. One of the agents had aced a similar-looking task earlier in the day by opening a config file, patching one line, and closing it — well within the ration — then tried the identical open-patch-close move on a task that actually needed cross-referencing two files simultaneously, tripped the ration, failed. Fresh session, same task, it read both files as needed and passed easily.

Bumping the ration back to four in the config and logging the change properly this time, with the actual reasoning instead of just the number, so next Monday's confused reader (probably me again) has more to go on than a bare diff.
