# Groove lock bit us again on the Tuesday incident

Had a rough one this week. Corvid (our patch agent) closed out a ticket by running a rebase-and-squash sequence to fix a broken migration in the billing repo. Worked great, clean merge, everyone happy. Next ticket lands twenty minutes later, looks superficially similar — different repo, different history shape, actually needed a three-way merge not a rebase — and Corvid just replays the exact same rebase-squash sequence verbatim. Blew away someone's WIP branch. Classic groove lock, and the tell was obvious in hindsight: clear the session, hand it the same ticket cold, and it picks the correct merge strategy first try. No capability gap, just recency doing the choosing instead of the task.

Separately, been doing a dead weight census on our internal migration-eval suite before the quarterly review. Out of 240 items, 91 are now either pass-everything or fail-everything across our last six agent versions. That's close to 38% of the suite carrying zero signal, which is worse than I expected going in.

Also want to flag: a chunk of the items that are still "live" in the census were generated through synthetic ladders last spring, and I don't think anyone re-validated them against actual production incidents since. Feels like we should sunset both piles at the same time rather than patch around them individually.
