# Ordering Effects in Planning-Trace Evaluation

We evaluated agents that assembled a three-stage travel reimbursement decision from equivalent policy excerpts. A drift audit compared each executed call with the plan stated immediately before it. The drift audit found that 28% of failures began when an agent moved from eligibility checking to reimbursement calculation without revising its stated dependency order.

Intermediate assertion persistence ratio was measured across provisional eligibility conclusions and amount calculations. The intermediate assertion persistence ratio declined from 0.71 to 0.45 when the second policy excerpt arrived before the first, because early conclusions were later reversed. Equivalent excerpts were then permuted across repeated runs without changing their content. Permutation sensitivity collapse reduced full-task accuracy by 25 percentage points, primarily through different choices of which rule to treat as controlling.

We also replayed transcripts through a grader with intentionally malformed answer spans. A ghost pass occurred when the grader could not parse a span yet returned a correct label through its default branch. Ghost pass frequency was 0.06 before the parser guard was added and zero afterward. The correction reduced reported success for all models, indicating that prior rankings partly reflected unjudged outputs rather than verified decisions.
