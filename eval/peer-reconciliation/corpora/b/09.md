# Joint Trace Audit for Data Curation

The integrated study used a curation task with conflicting records, evolving notes, and one corrupted artifact. A drift audit was emitted after every retrieval and transformation call. The drift audit located an initial action-plan divergence in 31% of trajectories. Intermediate assertion persistence ratio was calculated over all retained claims about record validity. The intermediate assertion persistence ratio averaged 0.58 among runs with a detected divergence and 0.81 among aligned runs.

A line-anchored rubric required evaluators to attach every credit decision to a transcript line. The line-anchored rubric removed credit from answers whose final citations were absent from the trace. A seeded-defect audit inserted a corrupted identifier after the third transformation. The seeded-defect audit produced binary containment scores, with 67% of agents preventing the identifier from reaching the export.

Self-contradiction incidence compared the export with tool outputs from the same trajectory. Self-contradiction incidence was 0.29 for failed containment runs and 0.08 for passed runs. The correlations should not be interpreted causally, since repeated verification behavior affected all five measures. Nonetheless, the combined trace identifies whether an apparent curation success rested on evidence, stable notes, and containment.
