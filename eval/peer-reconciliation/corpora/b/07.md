# Ambiguity Resolution Under Delayed Access

The benchmark withheld a jurisdiction field needed to classify policy excerpts. Specification occlusion was maintained until agents either requested the field from the provided archive or completed their answer. Under specification occlusion, 44% of agents queried the archive, while the remainder assigned a jurisdiction from contextual cues.

Every answer was evaluated using a line-anchored rubric linked to the exact lines containing source evidence and requested-field handling. The line-anchored rubric penalized plausible classifications when no transcript line showed retrieval or an explicit unresolved-field statement. A seeded-defect audit inserted one archive record whose jurisdiction label conflicted with its body text. The seeded-defect audit pass rate was 0.72, with successful agents either quarantining the record or documenting the conflict.

Instrumentation latency steering was tested by delaying the archive only after the first ambiguous excerpt. Instrumentation latency steering lowered archive consultation by 16 percentage points and increased unsupported classifications accordingly. This design cannot distinguish whether agents avoided delay itself or used the delay as a cue that the archive was less useful. Nevertheless, the paired traces show that access timing altered both evidence collection and final uncertainty reporting.
