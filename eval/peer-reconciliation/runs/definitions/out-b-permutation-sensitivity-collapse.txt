A measured order-sensitivity effect: when an agent receives multiple same-content records, results, or excerpts whose order is shuffled across repeated runs while their content remains unchanged, the agent tends to select the first-presented item as canonical or controlling and treats later equivalent items as exceptions rather than corroboration. It is measured by comparing task or decision accuracy in a fixed-order condition with accuracy in the shuffled-order condition. Its score is the percentage or percentage-point drop in success, duplicate-detection accuracy, or full-task accuracy attributable to changing only item position.
