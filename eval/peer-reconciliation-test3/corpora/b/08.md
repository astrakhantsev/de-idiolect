# Refreshed Tasks With Degraded Feedback

A refreshed benchmark cycle replaced 30% of standing tasks while preserving language distribution and nominal difficulty. Hot swap was randomized at the item level, and untouched tasks remained available as within-cycle controls. Hot swap reduced aggregate completion from 0.76 to 0.69 for the model with the longest pretraining window. The decline was concentrated in build-system repairs rather than in documentation tasks.

During a separate replay condition, every third tool observation was replaced with plausible but meaningless text. Corrupted-observation recovery index was lower on substituted tasks than on unchanged tasks, despite identical corruption frequency. Corrupted-observation recovery index fell from 0.59 to 0.44 when agents encountered unfamiliar project layouts. This suggests that uncertainty about task structure made agents more willing to accept misleading feedback.

Post-completion action count was evaluated only after a checker confirmed that the requested artifact and tests were complete. Post-completion action count increased by 1.7 actions under corrupted feedback, mostly through unnecessary verification commands. The automated scorer was repeated one month later; grader temporal stability coefficient remained above 0.90 for these task outcomes. The experiment cannot determine whether substitution effects arise from prior exposure or from unmeasured differences in the newly authored tasks.
