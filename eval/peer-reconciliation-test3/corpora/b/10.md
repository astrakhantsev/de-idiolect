# Selector Exchange in Unreliable Replays

This experiment replayed newly authored and standing benchmark items while tool results were semantically degraded at prespecified action boundaries. Corrupted-observation recovery index was computed separately by task provenance and selector assignment. Corrupted-observation recovery index improved from 0.42 to 0.63 after selector replacement, largely because the replacement issued explicit retries before editing. Grader temporal stability coefficient was estimated from repeated submissions of every final answer across two collection dates. Grader temporal stability coefficient was 0.88, indicating that the observed recovery difference was not explained by unstable scoring alone.

Reference trajectory alignment score was derived from curator-authored routes for all replayed tasks. Reference trajectory alignment score increased under the replacement selector even when the final answer was unchanged. Component substitution elasticity was negative on standing tasks, reducing total actions by 12%, but positive on newly authored tasks by 5%. Component substitution elasticity therefore depended on whether the selector encountered familiar task structure.

Cross-lingual instruction fidelity was evaluated in a Spanish-instruction subset embedded in the same replay corpus. Cross-lingual instruction fidelity remained 0.09 below the English control despite the recovery improvement. Cold start was measured before each selector run and was balanced by randomized worker assignment. Cold start explained less than 1% of variation in total action count. Goal-revision compliance rate was collected from a smaller interrupted subset. Goal-revision compliance rate showed no reliable selector effect, although the interval was wide.
