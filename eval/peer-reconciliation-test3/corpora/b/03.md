# Support Ablation and Item Refresh

The RepairBench prompts contained a compact task outline, one solved demonstration, output-format reminders, and localized hints about likely files. Each ingredient was removed separately while all repositories and tests were held fixed. Prompt scaffolding dependence measure increased sharply when the solved demonstration was withheld from the smaller systems. Prompt scaffolding dependence measure changed little when only output-format reminders were removed, suggesting that the effect was not driven solely by answer serialization.

A second evaluation cycle replaced 25% of the benchmark with newly authored defects matched by language, test count, and estimated repair length. Hot swap was applied without exposing item identities to model operators or annotators. Hot swap produced a 14.1-point score drop for the legacy checkpoint, whereas the instruction-tuned checkpoint dropped 3.7 points. On the substituted subset, hot swap also increased variance across random seeds, consistent with less reliable generalization than aggregate scores implied.

The new defects were authored by the same internal team that maintained the standing suite, so stylistic continuity may have reduced the magnitude of the replacement effect. Results should therefore be interpreted as sensitivity to this refresh procedure rather than a comprehensive estimate of prior exposure.
