# Our pivot check numbers looked great until I checked budget bleed

Posted a pivot check score of 88% last month and got some nice feedback in the team channel. Then I actually looked at what those trials cost and felt less good about it. The agent does discard the stale objective and pursue the revised one, so the check passes, but it gets there by throwing away enormous amounts of partial work and starting over almost from scratch every single time instead of salvaging anything reusable from before the reversal.

So I ran the budget bleed numbers on the same trial set. Average of 34% of total tokens on those pivot trials went into work that got discarded outright, versus about 9% on trials with no mid-task instruction change. That's a real cost difference even though the pivot check metric itself doesn't see it — you can nail the pivot and still be lighting money on fire to do it. I think we've been treating pivot check as sufficient evidence of good behavior when it's really only measuring the endpoint, not the path.

Also worth mentioning quit sense here because a few of the high-bleed trials weren't clean pivots at all — the agent kept a couple of stray tool calls going after the revised objective was already achieved, like it forgot to check whether it was done. Not unbounded looping exactly, more like two extra unnecessary verification passes tacked onto an already-finished task. Small thing but it shows up in both the token bill and the transcript noise.
