The package's results answer to two different baselines — plus one absolute pre-registered threshold with no baseline arm — and it never sets them side by side. They are not interchangeable: read a number against the wrong comparator and its meaning inverts.

## Baseline A — searching in your own coined vocabulary

The baseline a reader reaches for by default: off-the-shelf deep research, or a naive ask, run in the terms a community minted for itself. Against A, the package's measured cell and its documented record returned zero recovery with confident wrong nulls. Across eight model configurations (two families, four capability tiers), none recovered an opaque coinage's owner from the bare term (ENTRY §1, CLAIMS C05 — measured, n = 1 concept). On the project's own record, three headline novelty claims were retracted, each preceded by a confident null: a novelty search in the project's own vocabulary that found nothing and looked thorough (ENTRY §2, CLAIMS C07–C08). The A-side evidence is thin and overlapping — the measured probe covers a single concept drawn from that same project record, and the three retractions are event history, not a recall evaluation — so no pooled recall figure exists; the package's one same-item coined-term comparison (the four-coinage pilot) is component-level and is stated under "What this page is not" below.

## Baseline B — a frontier model already handed equal information

The "no margin over a direct frontier ask" result does not score against A. Its baseline is a frontier model given the same frozen excerpts and the same 18-document corpus (ENTRY §5.3, EXPERIMENT-LOG E3) — an equal-information grounded direct ask, not any off-the-shelf tool. That baseline ranked the true owner literature 1–2, and against it the pipeline's mechanical form earned no measured margin on that cell (CLAIMS C29, C37). B is the demanding comparison the entry holds itself to; the entry's stated case rests on process properties, not on beating B.

## Write-time versus read-time (scope note)

Where a single writing authority spans all the artifacts that will later be read together, the remedy can act at write time — vocabulary control through schema slots, reserved-term registries, coinage discipline — before the terms harden. The entry's coin-time interception (ENTRY §4.2) is that kind of control, scoped to the one corpus this project has authority over — its own future coinages — and at submission it was a proposal, not a tested capability (CLAIMS C26 grades the hook argued and unbuilt; its implementation is a separately labeled post-submission addition). The outward workflow targets the opposite regime, defined precisely by the absence of any such authority: the corpus is already written, the coinages are fixed, and no convention we author reaches the people who wrote them. That is why, there, the intervention has to move to read time — the only point still under the reader's control. This is a scope statement about where each remedy applies — a judgment, not a measured comparison, and not a new result.
