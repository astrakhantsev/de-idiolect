# The vocabulary seam: why cross-community knowledge doesn't compound, and a lazy way to bridge it

*An FLF Epistemic Case Study Competition entry — a workflow proposal based on a diagnosis from prior work, a documented case study of the failure on the author's own project, a small runnable prototype, and exploratory experiments. Layers addressed: Ingestion and Structure.*

**Abstract.** Knowledge fails to compound across people partly for a mechanical reason: groups coin private vocabulary, search under it, and cannot find each other's work. This failure has been measured repeatedly, from 1985 (recall around 20% where searchers had stipulated they needed 75%) to 2016 (9% average retrieval of relevant constructs in modern digital-library search). We document the same failure in our own 2026 research project using agent-assisted search: three retracted headline novelty claims across two programs, each preceded by a confident null from a search in the project's own vocabulary. The tooling changed across four decades; the recall failure did not.

We propose detecting community-local terms as they naturally occur, generating a term-free operational definition of each, and searching with the definition rather than the term, using typed relations without ever building a canonical vocabulary. The pieces exist in prior work, credited by name; what we did not find is the assembly as one system. And because definitions are generated on demand and disagreement is stored as typed links, the shared layer can accumulate across communities without requiring agreement — a design property, not yet a demonstrated one.

We tested the pieces at small scale, most against pre-registered endpoints. On the competition's eggs case, the term-free definition retrieves the owning specialist literature that the lay question misses (rank 1 versus 4–8). A pilot on our own record locates where the method earns its keep: coinages whose surface words hide, or misdirect from, the concept. And the pipeline's first full run — a sealed test on a synthetic two-community benchmark — held detection precision 1.00, made zero false matches, and typed every asserted relation correctly, but resolved only 5 of 10 planted pairs against a pre-registered target of 7, with the shortfall at its own generation-quality gates rather than wrong matches. The pre-registered negatives redraw the test map: (1) public synonym pairs cannot test tools like this, because any pair prominent enough for a curated thesaurus — *Kawasaki disease* / *mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome*, one pair from our screen — is already reconciled in the models' training data; and (2) generation fails first at faithfulness — definitions subtly distort what the concept claims — a failure our round-trip check catches before any search runs.

The evidence so far supports the diagnosis, the routing mechanism, and conservative matching — not yet live cross-community compounding. What we submit is accordingly a workflow design, not a retrieval algorithm: its case rests on deterministic triggering (our failed novelty searches ran under standing instructions to check the field's vocabulary first — installed, and the failure happened anyway; a hook fires regardless), blind generation, verification, and durable reuse of each verified mapping — not on out-retrieving a frontier model given the same corpus, a margin our own baseline did not find. Throughout, we distinguish what is measured, argued, and unbuilt.

## 1. Introduction

FLF's first-stated limitation of today's tools is that they "mostly produce single-user artifacts tuned to one investigator's context, not the kind that travel, combine, or survive (especially adversarial) scrutiny." We ask a narrower question beneath it: *mechanically, why doesn't knowledge combine across the people who hold it?*

A large part of the answer is **vocabulary**. Groups working apart coin their own terms and give ordinary words local meanings. When one group searches in its own words, it misses other groups' work on the same thing. This has been measured repeatedly:

- **Blair & Maron (1985, *CACM* 28(3):289–299.)** In a litigation-support corpus, lawyers "stipulated that they must be able to retrieve at least 75 percent of all the documents relevant to a given request"; measured recall was **about 20 percent**. The paper explains why the gap is invisible: "they have seen that the proportion of relevant documents in the retrieved set (i.e., Precision) is quite good (around 80 percent)." You can observe precision but not recall, so a clean search feels thorough even when it misses most of what exists.
- **Larsen & Bong (2016, *MIS Quarterly* 40(3):529–551.)** "The name of that construct can neither be trusted to elicit high recall … nor high precision." Doctoral students using full-text search retrieved **9%** of relevant constructs on average (per-task as low as **3%**); in the meta-analytic follow-up, "**83%** of participants would arrive at the conclusion that the relationship had not been tested at all." Extending Swanson (1986), they write that "the vast majority of *discovered* knowledge is hidden from the individual researcher by the very nature of the search process itself" and note that "the renaming of an existing construct (jangle fallacy) may increase the *perceived novelty* of a construct."
- **Furnas, Landauer, Gomez & Dumais (1987, *CACM* 30(11):964–971.)** The probability that two people spontaneously choose the same term for the same thing was **.07–.18**. Naming divergence is the default. (Their base rate is untrained people naming everyday objects; the magnitude transfers by analogy, not measurement.)
- The failure's name is old: the **jangle fallacy** — treating two things as different because they are named differently — is **Kelley (1927, pp. 63–64)**; its twin *jingle* is **Thorndike (1904) quoting Aikins**.

These are not merely measurements of outdated tools. The 1985 number comes from full-text retrieval, the 2016 number from digital-library search by trained researchers, and §2 from agent-assisted search in 2026. The instruments improved across four decades; the recall failure recurred.

**From FLF's problem statement to this proposal in two steps.** Artifacts fail to travel, combine, or survive scrutiny because they are *indexed under private keys*: people search in the vocabulary they coined. Other communities' work cannot be found, built on, or confronted. Scrutiny cannot locate the prior art your claim collides with, and you cannot locate theirs. The cause suggests the countermeasure: describe the concept in community-neutral language and search with that description. The same operation points in two directions. **Outward**: find another community's work (§4.1; §5.1 measures the within-community half; §5.5 runs a sealed synthetic cross-community evaluation; live-community deployment is owed, §7). **Inward**: let a field's prior work reach your coinages before you commit to them (§4.2).

**Why better models don't close the seam on their own** *(argued; one small measured leg).* Nothing in the training pipeline rewards a model for volunteering "your term for this is not the field's term." A more capable model briefed on a community's documents can become a more fluent mirror of its vocabulary and return a better-argued, better-cited null. Presentation improves while the underlying failure remains. The incentive gradient is one-sided for Blair & Maron's reason: a wrong volunteered equivalence is visible; an omission produces no signal.

The nearest measurements concern framing agreement and presupposition handling rather than terminology, so they are analogies, not measurements of this failure. Preference data rewards matching the user's frame: "both humans and preference models (PMs) prefer convincingly-written sycophantic responses over correct ones a non-negligible fraction of the time" (**Sharma et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.13548**). Mirroring worsens with scale: "Larger LMs repeat back a dialog user's preferred answer ('sycophancy')" (**Perez et al. 2022, arXiv:2212.09251**). A coinage also embeds a presupposition of the kind QA systems handle poorly: models "struggle when predicting whether a presupposition is factually correct," largely because of "difficulty in retrieving relevant evidence passages" (**CREPE, ACL 2023, arXiv:2211.17257**) — again, a recall problem. We do not claim that the terminology-specific failure *worsens* with capability: our probe returned "not shown" (materials linked). We claim only that it did not self-correct in the tested case and cannot be assumed to do so.

One small measurement of our own points the same way. Across eight model configurations (two families, four capability tiers), no model recovered one opaque coinage from §2 from the bare term — recall flat at floor, with stronger models scattering into confidently wrong fields. A term-free definition of the same concept recovered the true owner at the frontier models. This is one concept, not a scaling law, but it matches the mechanism: capability helps recall through the neutral-definition channel, not around it.

## 2. The case: the failure, documented on this project's own record

Our strongest evidence for §1 is our project's documented history, reconstructed from raw documents in the linked [case study](https://github.com/astrakhantsev/de-idiolect/blob/main/receipts/idiolect-trap-case-study.md). Load-bearing owners are verified in the case study's graded ledger — against primary text except where marked (secondary), as for Raiffa & Schlaifer.

Two consecutive research programs. **Program A** tried to build a *source-dependence detector*: estimate how many effectively independent sources stand behind a multi-source verdict. **Program B** asked whether cross-judge LLM error correlation is locked in the weights or reducible by elicitation. Both used pre-registration, adversarial cross-model review, and repeated prior-art searches.

Across ~7,000 model calls and some twenty experimental legs (Program B alone), the project produced **roughly twenty vocabulary-level renamings**. The ledger grades each row, and they are not one class: ~3 genuine recall misses of objects established fields already owned, ~10 knowing borrowings (the field's term cited at or near coinage), ~4 self-errors, and ~4 rows still asserted or defective — the last excluded from any verified-renaming count. Three became headline novelty claims before retraction:

The pattern *(measured, on the record)*:

- **Every retraction was preceded by a confident null** — a novelty search in the project's own vocabulary that found nothing and appeared thorough. This is §1's observability asymmetry in practice.
- **Knowing the failure mode did not prevent it.** The day-one scoping declared a terminology sweep owed; the pre-mortem named the risk; a mid-program sweep concluded "novelty survives" one week before both Program-A claims were found predated. Every sweep searched the coined vocabulary over overlapping literature.
- **Same-briefed agreement supplied no independent evidence.** A "six agents out of six agree" verdict was one search run six times. A refuter assigned a disjoint literature overturned one consensus in minutes, pointing to 1990s psychometrics after three agents had searched only post-2023 preprints.

**The countermeasure, piloted on the same record** *(measured (exploratory))*. After the retractions, we generated an anonymized term-free definition for four coinages from era-gated internal excerpts. Only documents predating each term's retraction were allowed, so definitions could not inherit the answers. Blind models then named the concept and its owner. We compared the definition arm with a coined-term control and sealed predictions per case. The control received the term as the project used it — e.g. "the cold-start operating requirement for the audit unit" — making its misrouting realistic; §5.3 later tested a truly bare one-term control.

For concreteness, here are the opening sentences of the frozen definition generated for "operating requirement," the artifact at the proposal's center (full texts in the materials):

> This is a quantitative specification stating how accurate a measuring procedure must be before using its output is better than not using it at all. It applies wherever a pipeline needs a numeric setting — here, a multiplier that scales pooled evidence — and one may either estimate that setting separately for each case or fall back to a single fixed value used for every case.

Ordinary words, with no field or method names; this paragraph recovered the owner hidden by the coinage.

| the coinage | opacity | coined-term control | definition arm | cell verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "operating requirement" | opaque | **actively misrouted** — "audit unit" → accounting, "cold-start" → recommender systems | **owner recovered**: Raiffa & Schlaifer 1961, exact, 2 of 3 draws | **clean — the one comparative cell** |
| `m*` | opaque symbol, textbook concept | went, plausibly, to rater-sample-size literatures | never reached Clemen & Winkler | confounded — definition drifted from the construct |
| "the signal, not the cut…" | transparent slogan | reached the owner's territory *from the slogan alone* (Murphy 1973; Blackwell) | same territory; no separation | confounded — definition inverted the claim's polarity |
| "read the enumerations, not the votes" | transparent phrase | one Delphi mention | named **Ho, Hull & Srihari 1994** in two independent draws | confounded against a mis-mapped answer key — see below |

Four caveats:

- The definition arm's advantage is **union-level only**: the union of draws reached the owner's family on 2 of 4 concepts versus the control's 1 of 4; per-draw medians tied.
- The era gate is retraction-dated, not discovery-dated: excerpts could not inherit the retractions, but might contain owner clues predating retraction. The later end-to-end cell froze pre-discovery excerpts (§5.3).
- The comparison varies information content as well as vocabulary: a definition carries more propositional content than a term (§5.1's ablation isolates that specificity effect). The pilot is discovery evidence, not an isolation of neutrality.
- The sharpest datum came from the *confounded* fourth cell and is candidate-discovery evidence, not a clean comparison. The definition layer surfaced **Ho, Hull & Srihari (1994, *IEEE TPAMI* 16(1):66–75)** as the true owner of the coinage's combination mechanics. The project's three earlier prior-art passes (model-run searches, human-audited) had missed it, and its answer key — the internal glossary row recording that coinage's believed owner — was mis-mapped. The author verified it against the primary text.

The generalizable reading *(measured (exploratory))* is that the definition layer's value tracked **the semantic transparency of the coinage**. For an opaque coinage over a non-obvious owner, the definition recovered what the term hid while the surface words misled. A transparent slogan carried the concept itself. When the field was already known at coinage, the problem was a missed citation rather than a hidden field. The supported regime is narrow: **de-idiolection earns its place on opaque coinages over non-obvious owners**. This is a scope hypothesis from a four-case pilot (per-case predictions sealed, cross-case pattern read after the fact), not a validated rule; a real pipeline should triage coinages by opacity first.

This remains a private project's history. The coinages and their public owners are citable, and each verification-graded row in the case study carries its stated grade (primary-read where so marked); the narrative is a documented account, not "I predicted X" evidence. Full reconstruction, term ledger (including its defective rows), and test-case assessment: [the case study](https://github.com/astrakhantsev/de-idiolect/blob/main/receipts/idiolect-trap-case-study.md).

## 3. Why the shared-vocabulary fix failed before, and what changed

The obvious fix — one shared vocabulary to which everyone maps — has repeatedly failed at scale (Cyc, the Semantic Web's universal-ontology wave, interlingua machine translation). Selected critiques suggest recurring causes — an argued synthesis over selected cases, not a demonstrated common cause:

- **Acquisition cost** — the knowledge-acquisition bottleneck (Feigenbaum's term, via **Hoekstra 2010, *Semantic Web* 1(1)**; Feigenbaum's original is (secondary)). Hand-building the resource is expensive.
- **Maintenance cost** — Hoekstra 2010's **knowledge-reengineering bottleneck**: reuse is "a continuous relation of trust"; schemas drift, and staying current is a recurring cost.
- **Incentives** — **Doctorow, "Metacrap" (2001)**: "People are lazy" (and "people lie").
- **Coordination** — **Shirky (2003)**: "you can't force an agreement to exist where none actually does."
- **Funding collapses** — the ALPAC report (1966) (secondary), cited as a historical datum.

The pattern is **universal + manual + build-once**. Each post-mortem suggests a different escape: drop universality (Shirky), drop manual acquisition (Cyc's lesson), or fund continual re-curation (Hoekstra). ("Every failed project" is not claimable; a full case matrix has not been built.)

**What changed** is an economic argument (unmeasured), narrower than the diagnosis. These bottlenecks were largely *marginal-cost* problems: writing and maintaining one more concept's definition. LLM generation makes that cheap. It addresses acquisition directly and maintenance only where regeneration can replace re-curation. The architecture (§4) avoids requiring agreement, addressing incentives and coordination by design rather than cost. We do not price the system's own lifecycle costs — detection, verification, provenance, and dispute handling — here (§7). The failure cannot be assumed to self-correct with scale (§1), but the countermeasure may benefit from scale twice: equivalence detection should improve with models (expected, unmeasured), while generation reduces the costs identified by these critiques. "This time is different" means "the unaffordable thing is now cheap," not "the models are smart enough now."

## 4. The proposed workflow

### 4.1 Three stages, outward

A lazy service rather than a standard: run one concept at a time, on demand, separately for each community.

1. **Detection.** Flag words used in a group-local way: coinages and ordinary words with a special local sense. *(Automatic term recognition / novel-sense detection; prior work in §6.)*
2. **Constrained definition.** Generate a short, self-contained, operational definition of the term *as used here* — the kind of thing it names, its inputs and outputs, what it asserts, and when it applies — written **only in a small fixed vocabulary of ordinary words plus simple notation, with no names of people, methods, or fields.** The constraint removes local lexical cues, making the definition more community-neutral. Whether ordinary words remove *enough* is a **hypothesis** (tested only in §5.1 and the experiment log's ablation).
3. **Definition-mediated matching.** Search across communities with the definition rather than the term; verify candidates pairwise; and label each relation with SKOS names taken verbatim (`skos:exactMatch`, `skos:broadMatch`, `skos:narrowMatch`, `skos:relatedMatch`) — local names for standard relations would repeat the error being fixed. Abstention and "no match despite surface similarity" are first-class outcomes. Broad/narrow direction is explicit (subject = the narrower side). Matching runs over *supplied* corpora; discovering which communities to search is a further, unbuilt stage, currently manual. Every measurement in §5 uses supplied or answer-aware corpora.

The two properties the architecture is built on — live cross-community matching and an enforced wordlist — are exactly the unrun ones; per-stage implementation status is tabulated in the appendix. (An interactive architecture diagram with per-node evidence status is in the supplementary materials ‹URL›.)

**Two design decisions, from the experimental record** *(both graded proposal; motivating results in §5.3–§5.4)*:

- **(i) Split the definition's two roles.** Retrieval keys may be cheap, lossy, and plural; only verification requires a *faithful representation*, checked for round-trip fidelity. We propose a multi-key union (raw term + restated question + definition), but it remains conjectural: the first frozen fusion cell was null — no union beat the best single key, and fusion promoted the misroute document (§5.4) — while recall, false-positive load, and verification cost at scale remain unevaluated. Fidelity-before-retrieval remains the default ordering. Under the split, lossy keys may fetch *candidates*, but no mapping advances to verification or publication without a gate-passing representation.
- **(ii) The generator is replaceable behind a contract.** Any implementation — model, prompting strategy, extractive selection, or human — satisfies the architecture if its output passes the fidelity checklist (round-trip entailment + external-name ban) *and* the stage-2 lexical constraint. The contract is the design; no implementation, including this prototype's, has demonstrated full compliance. One step has been measured: regeneration guided by a checklist extracted from source excerpts cleared the fidelity half of the contract two-for-two where unguided generation failed (§5.4). Wordlist enforcement remains unbuilt.

**Naming.** We call the operation **de-idiolect** (verb; "de-idiolection" for the noun): remove a community's local vocabulary from a concept's description. In the field's terms, this is on-demand definition-mediated cross-community concept search; the closest prior systems are EDC, GenOM, and SciCo-Radar (§6). Following §4.2's coin-time practice, the term comes with its translation and nearest prior work attached; the project's records call the failure it counters the *idiolect trap*.

**Two design commitments.**

- **(i) No universal resource.** Definitions are local and lazy, with no agreement-first canonical ontology. The frame is **Star & Griesemer's boundary objects (1989)**: communities that never converge on one classification can still cooperate through shared translation devices. The constrained definitions are manufactured boundary objects. **Bowker & Star (1999)** — "there is no such thing as a natural or universal classification system" — informs the design.
- **(ii) The shared layer, when present, is a derived, regenerable cache — never a canonical merge.** It is an optional decentralized commons of definitions and verified typed mappings with provenance, re-derivable from source texts. Because regeneration is stochastic, entries must be versioned (source hashes, generator and prompt identity, verifier provenance). Re-derivability covers only *candidate* artifacts; verification judgments, attestations, and dispute records are durable history and must persist. Forks coexist, and disagreement is a typed link. The intended compounding property — accumulate without agreement — is a **design property, not a demonstrated outcome**. Prior owners of the stance include emergent semantics (Aberer & Cudré-Mauroux 2004) (secondary), pay-as-you-go dataspaces (Franklin, Halevy & Maier 2005) (secondary), and, recently, Relink (arXiv:2601.07192) — "reason-and-construct" over "build-then-reason."

**One shared artifact is allowed, and the distinction matters.** The fixed defining wordlist (LDOCE-style: small, domain-general, comparatively low-maintenance — revised only a few times in decades) *is* shared. We exclude an *authoritative, agreement-first domain concept resource* — a canonical ontology, termbase, or glossary on which communities must converge. The optional commons (commitment ii) remains permitted because it is derived, versioned, non-authoritative, and forkable. The invariant is "no canonical merge," not "nothing shared." Generic defining wordlists survived for decades largely because they require little curation, avoiding the economics blamed in §3's critiques for domain-resource failures. This is an argument, not a measurement.

### 4.2 The same three stages, turned inward

§2's *verified* failures sort one-to-one onto the pipeline's stages *(small counts, stated)*. The ~3 recall misses were missed coining moments (**detection**, stage 1). The ~4 self-errors were definition or estimand drift, including the retracted headline that substituted one quantity's role for another's (**definition**, stage 2). **Verification** — stage 3's checking step — failed in provenance: both retractions checked against primary text used real quotes with defective citations, and one work appeared under conflicting identifiers. Across ~85 recorded review findings, none identified an erroneous computed value. The failures lie in the layer connecting claims to sources, where this pipeline operates.

Turned inward, the pipeline suggests three interventions in a researcher's workflow — all **proposals grounded in that taxonomy, not tested capabilities**:

- **Coin-time interception** (detection): when a term is minted, generate its neutral definition and propose the field's nearest existing terms, recording the translation while it is cheapest. §4.1's naming note applies this practice to the proposal's own term.
- **Claim-time estimand self-audit** (definition): regenerate the definition of what a claim *measures* and compare it with what the instrument *computes* — the error class that inverted Program A's headline.
- **Cite-time provenance check** (verification): before a load-bearing citation ships, open the primary, check the venue, and read past the ellipsis. This now has a measured yield and cost on the project's record *(measured)*. In a seeded sample of 100 model-proposed citation occurrences, roughly half to two-thirds survived verification depending on strictness (66/100 at the lenient primary; 43/91 at the strictest sensitivity, which also drops nine ineligible candidates; full contingency detail in the materials). There were zero confirmed fabrications and zero major bibliographic mismatches among checkable cases. Attrition came mostly from publisher access walls and attribution precision, at roughly one search and two fetches per work. Reachability constrained checking, arguing for durable verified mappings (the commons, §4.1) over repeated verification by each user.

Two caveats apply. The retrospective version of coin-time interception was effectively run in §2 and was near-null on fresh cases. These are workflow designs with a measured scope boundary (the transparency gradient), not demonstrated rescues. The rescue framing also changes what counts as redundancy: this project queried *late, in its own vocabulary, with same-model fan-out*. The knowledge itself was reachable. For rescue, memorized knowledge is useful; the tool must trigger the lookup at the right time and in the right vocabulary. We do not claim that it finds things a frontier model does not know, which is also why it can be simple.

**The minimal implementation, and what the architecture adds.** For the anchoring case (owner long-established), the minimal inward tool is a *hook*: at coin, claim, or cite time, send the frozen excerpts to a separate model call — "what is this called, and who owns it?" — and surface the answer. Does the proposal reduce to that? The *prompt* half has been measured on this record: the agents running §2's "novelty survives" sweep already had standing instructions to *search the field's vocabulary before committing*. The instruction was installed, and the failure still occurred. A standing instruction must win an attention contest every turn (§1's incentive argument); a hook fires deterministically. The hook half is argued and unbuilt. The architecture adds four elements, each addressing a measured failure:

- **Verification**: unverified flags can export fabricated owners — two-of-two checked retractions had real quotes with defective citations; the fidelity check caught two-of-two drifted definitions.
- **Blindness**: the checking call receives only the frozen source excerpts, with no project conclusions, candidate owners, or session context. Same-context checks produced §2's confident nulls, and genuinely blind calls required harness-level isolation.
- **Grounding**: a direct ask can reach only what the weights already connect; post-cutoff or genuinely parallel seams require retrieval over the other community's texts (§7).
- **The commons**: a hook's flag is an ephemeral single-user artifact — FLF's stated limitation — while a verified mapping recorded once can travel.

## 5. Experiments

*Protocol-level detail for every experiment in this section — endpoints, operating characteristics, per-arm observations, bounds — is in the experiment log ([EXPERIMENT-LOG.md](https://github.com/astrakhantsev/de-idiolect/blob/main/EXPERIMENT-LOG.md)); this section keeps the headline results.*

### 5.1 A retrieval check on FLF's eggs case

Can a definition, rather than a term, serve as the retrieval key? A small published [prototype](https://github.com/astrakhantsev/de-idiolect/tree/main/prototype) measures this on FLF's eggs case, offline and deterministically, for three specialist concepts a lay *"are eggs bad for me?"* question circles but cannot name. The concepts and their owners are hand-supplied — an oracle condition: this tests routing, not discovery. Rank of the owning sub-field's best document among 15 (lower is better):

| concept (owning sub-field) | naive question | raw term | **constrained definition** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| hyper-responder (lipidology) | 5 | 1 | **1** |
| apolipoprotein B particle number (cardiology biomarkers) | 4 | 1 | **1** |
| isocaloric substitution model (nutrition-epi methods) | 8 | 1 | **1** |

The naive question buries the owner at 4–8; the term-free definition (prompt-constrained — the enforced wordlist is not implemented) brings it to rank 1, matching the raw term the lay asker doesn't have. The isolated effect is **specificity, not vocabulary-neutrality**: the ablation varied that axis and found no rank difference (experiment log; inconclusive), and reaching a *second* community is untested here (§7). n = 3 concepts, one case, one model; full limits in the log.

### 5.2 A cross-community cell on curated vocabulary (pre-registered; negative)

A pre-registered cell on a curated-vocabulary pair (MeSH) triggered its success rule — at a threshold later shown to sit at chance, so it supports no retrieval claim; claims briefly layered on it were retracted under review. The search for a second cell then failed for a structural reason: **curated synonyms are memorized by construction** — any pair prominent enough for a curated thesaurus is already reconciled in the models' training corpora, so a frontier model bridges it from weights (measured on the vocabularies, models, and cutoffs searched; generalization beyond them is argued, not measured). The candidate clean regimes narrow to **post-cutoff concepts and private coinages**; §2's pilot holds the one clean private-coinage cell. *(Measured.)*

### 5.3 An end-to-end cell on the project's own coinage (pre-registered; negative, with baseline)

A second pre-registered cell ran the generative path end-to-end on §2's "operating requirement," from frozen pre-discovery excerpts. **Both answer-blind definitions failed the frozen fidelity check on the same feature (prospectivity) before retrieval could be scored** — §2's definitional drift, replicated two-for-two on fresh generations and caught by the gate. Leak checks passed in every arm, so **fidelity, not leakage, is where generation first failed its contract** — the origin of §4.1's component contract. Separately, outside the pre-registration, the retrospective detection demo missed its endpoint (the coinage ranked ~224th).

A **grounded direct-model baseline** — the same excerpts and corpus, given to a frontier model — ranked the true owner literature 1–2. Against an equal-information direct ask, the pipeline's mechanical form earned **no measured margin on this cell** (the baseline had the corpus, so this does not map the memorized-vs-inferred boundary); the remaining case rests on §4.2's process properties. Post-gate retrieval observations, the Larsen & Bong precedent, and the full accounting are in the log.

### 5.4 Follow-up measurements on the failed stage (second frozen set)

A second frozen specification measured the gate and the candidate fix on the same cell (endpoints frozen before running). Four results: **the gate calibrates** — faithful definitions pass 4/4, seeded defects are caught 5/5 with correct attribution *(pre-registered; a non-degeneracy check on author-constructed materials, not a population false-positive estimate)*; **a cheap fix clears it** — regeneration guided by a checklist extracted from the source excerpts passes the gate 2/2 where unguided generation had failed 0/2 *(pre-registered; n = 1 per configuration, and a stricter prospectivity reading is arguably still unmet)*; **fidelity bought no routing** — the gate-passing definitions rank the owner no better than the drifted ones they replace *(measured (exploratory))*; and **union-of-keys returned a null** — no fusion beat the best single key, and every fusion promoted the misroute document *(pre-registered)*. The single-cell, answer-aware limits of §5.3 apply; full bounds are in the log. (A fourth measurement from the same set — provenance-verification yield on the project's citations — appears where it applies, §4.2.)

### 5.5 The peer-reconciliation evaluation, built and run (pre-registered; sealed held-out test)

We then implemented and ran the synthetic core of §7's continuation design: eight pre-registered pipeline versions on synthetic two-community corpora (10 planted pairs per key; no live-community claim), with cross-family blind generation and bilateral usage-based verification, under a train/test protocol with a sealed held-out key. **On the sealed key — the pipeline's first full run — it scored 5/10 with detection precision 1.00, zero false positives and zero false escalations, and all five hard relation assertions typed correctly; that is below its pre-registered bar of ≥ 7, at every pre-registered operating point.** Coverage (0.6) was the binding constraint: three pairs were lost at generation-stage artifact gates and one at quote validation. Across versions the binding layer moved from definition fidelity to generated-artifact gate survival, extending §5.3–§5.4's negative. Protocol, the sealed-key procedure, per-version record, and the five adversarial review rounds are in the log. *(Measured.)*

## 6. Prior work

Stated without project-local terms, the proposal is: *a pay-as-you-go, schema-last system for onomasiological cross-community concept search, in which automatic term recognition (including novel-sense detection) triggers on-demand definition generation in a controlled defining vocabulary, and the generated definition — not the term — is the retrieval representation for cross-community matching with SKOS-style typed relations, with no agreement-first canonical resource ever built.*

**The stages, and several sub-assemblies, exist in prior work; we did not find the full assembly as one system.** Two blind passes reproduced this map and found no fused system. The nearest whole-system efforts — **LAVOHA** (CEUR Vol-4177) and Battelle's **Vocabulary Switching System** (Niehoff & Mack 1984 evaluation of the system designed ~1980, with a 1976 Niehoff precursor) — build merged canonical resources, the opposite of the no-merge commitment, and neither combines an automatic detection trigger with definitions constrained to a controlled defining vocabulary (LAVOHA produces harmonized consensus definitions). The absence claim is bounded: the searches used one model family and one web surface, later passes kept surfacing closer neighbours (EDC, since read; GESIS cross-concordances/KoMoHe (secondary) — manual persistent crosswalks, the architectural opposite; Guo et al. 2024, arXiv:2311.09481, for detection), and the draft search ledger with its coverage gaps is linked in the appendix. The entry rests on the diagnosis, the documented case, the measurements, and attribution — not on novelty.

The credits, by stage. (Verification surfaces per citation follow the note at the References; per-item detail is in the linked dossiers. Items known only from search-run summaries are marked (secondary).)

- **The core pipeline** (generate definitions → embed → match → type relations) exists in prior work:
  - **SciCo-Radar** (arXiv:2409.15113, Forer & Hope): LLM-generated context-dependent, relational definitions for cross-document concept coreference with typed relations (mentions pre-annotated).
  - **GenOM** (arXiv:2508.10703): definition-embedding as retrieval key with pairwise LLM verification, over existing ontologies.
  - Structurally closest, **EDC** (Zhang & Soh, arXiv:2404.03868): extract → define → canonicalize-by-definition-vector — the same shape as detect → define → match, but with a different purpose: a persisted self-generated knowledge-graph schema from one corpus (the master list this proposal refuses), without a detection trigger or controlled vocabulary.
  - **KGGen** (NeurIPS 2025): the same different-names matching, resolved through the canonical merge this proposal rejects. LLMs4OM and KROMA (both (secondary)) complete the landscape.
  - The pre-LLM ancestor of content-based rather than name-based matching is **CID** (Larsen & Bong 2016): construct identity by semantic similarity over measurement *items*, with names explicitly set aside, thresholded candidates for expert review, and automatic construct knowledge bases as the goal (Li & Larsen 2011/2013 (secondary)). CID matches existing curated item pools within one discipline; it has no detection trigger or generated definitions, and seeks a persistent curated resource.
- **Detection-as-trigger** is predated: Microsoft **US8589791B2 / US10552522B2** (unfamiliar-term detection → definition; one continuation family), **NeoN** (arXiv:2505.15426; neologism detection feeding LLM definitions), and the **AXOLOTL'24** line (novel senses → generated definitions).
- **The lazy / no-global-resource stance** is predated for single-community use: emergent semantics and dataspaces (§4.1), ad hoc terminography (Wright & Budin; Cabré — both (secondary)), and at least one shipped product providing lazy per-workspace LLM definitions (Confluence "Define" (secondary)). Atlan Sage (secondary) reportedly resolves conflicts into one canonical answer — the opposite philosophy and a useful contrast.
- **Two strips stay at "not found in the searches on record" (candidate status only — not the pitch):** (i) a controlled defining vocabulary for machine-generated cross-community definitions (all systems found use free text) — our ablation of whether the constraint helps retrieval was inconclusive (experiment log), weakening rather than strengthening this point; (ii) the full assembly as one system — detector-triggered, constrained-vocabulary definitions used as cross-community matching keys with typed relations and no canonical merge — per the paragraph above.

## 7. Discussion: FLF's four questions

- **Would it help someone reason better about these cases?** On FLF's eggs case, §5.1 measures the definition placing the owning sub-literature at rank 1 where the lay question buries it, conditional on an oracle-supplied concept. The most immediately usable piece is the cite-time provenance check, measured on our own citations: 66/100 survived at the lenient endpoint with zero confirmed fabrications, at about one search and two fetches per work (§4.2). On a real research program, §2 records three retractions, each preceded by a confident null, and §4.2 identifies the three moments when the inward stages would have fired. (The programs also failed on separate technical grounds; the claim that earlier field vocabulary would have saved them is a counterfactual, stated as such.)
- **Does it generalize?** Only at small-prototype plus one-clean-cell strength, bounded by the transparency gradient: routing on eggs (§5.1 — a specificity effect; neutrality untested), one cross-domain owner recovered in the clean cell, and a three-decade-old owner recovered from a confounded cell (§2). The supported regime is opaque coinages over non-obvious owners. Stronger claims require more runs; we make none.
- **Does it scale with AI / compute? A mechanism with a boundary, not a measured result.** The naive worry ("won't better models just know all these links?") conflates memorization with inference. A stronger model memorizes more equivalences already stated somewhere: reconciled seams where the tool is redundant. On an unreconciled seam, there is nothing to memorize. A capable model can still *infer* equivalence when given both communities' texts, but that grounded inference is the define→match operation, not a shortcut around it. Capability may improve inference; recall still determines whether the other community's work surfaces at all (Blair & Maron: precision is observable, recall is not). The tool's value therefore concentrates on the coined / post-cutoff / genuinely-parallel frontier, and remains marginal over asking a frontier model directly with the same corpus access — a baseline that improves every generation. **The two §5 negatives bound this from both sides**: curated public pairs are memorized (§5.2); on one private-coinage cell, the equal-information direct ask matched or beat the pipeline's mechanical form (§5.3); and faithful definitions did not route better (§5.4). The remaining case rests on §4.2's process properties and candidate regimes unreached by these experiments. The §3 economics remains a hypothesis-grade companion and does not price other lifecycle costs.
- **Does it compound?** The architecture's criterion (§4.1) is reconciliation as a lazy service: a regenerable-cache commons where forks coexist and disagreement is a typed link, allowing accumulation without agreement. This directly addresses FLF's stated limitation. Not demonstrated: the prototype does not run the commons.

**The continuation evaluation, designed here and then run (§5.5).** The remaining claim must be tested through *peer reconciliation*: two live communities naming overlapping concepts without an established owner to defer to — e.g. post-2022 alignment vocabulary. §5.5 implemented the design's core properties on synthetic corpora: cross-family blind generation (a shared generator or context cannot manufacture the match), bilateral usage-based verification (never definition-against-definition, since two definitions passed through one wordlist mechanically resemble each other), first-class no-match verdicts, and output as three artifacts rather than a winning term (typed relation, shared core, per-side residues). Two properties wait for the live setting: attestation dates recorded as data with no priority computed, and a screening gate first — most well-known pairs should already be reconciled in corpora and weights, and a zero-survivor screen would itself reveal how quickly public vocabulary reconciles, within the candidate frame.

The held-out result (§5.5) bounds the design: the no-fabrication property transferred, but coverage fell to 0.6 and the pre-registered bar was missed. The next work, ordered by information per unit cost, is to harden generation-stage artifact survival on fresh corpora, where the held-out run lost coverage. Only then comes the live-community deployment described above: post-cutoff, wild rather than planted vocabulary pairs, beyond the reach of any synthetic result here.

**What this entry is.** The contribution of record is the diagnosis, the documented case, and the evaluation methodology for this tool class; the pipeline is the instrument those were measured on, not yet a working bridge. The submission's case rests on workflow properties — deterministic triggering, blindness, verification, durable reuse — not on out-retrieving a frontier model with the same corpus access (§5.3 found no such margin).

**What is built and what is not.**

- *Built and runnable*: candidate-term surfacing (runs; missed its retrospective demo endpoint in §5.3) and matching (real embedding retrieval; §5.1's measurement), offline and deterministic; live definition generation behind an operational interface (routing held; full contract compliance, including the enforced wordlist, not demonstrated); relation typing scaffolded in the §5.1 prototype without a live measured run there. The separate synthetic harness in §5.5 measured bilateral verification and relation typing end-to-end, but real-community deployment remains unrun.
- *Run once, exploratory*: the naming pilot (§2), vocabulary ablation (experiment log), end-to-end cell (§5.3), and follow-up measurement set (§5.4).
- *Not built*: a live-generation superiority evaluation on a real corpus, scaling beyond n = 3 / one case / one model, precision–recall with relevance judgments, the inward workflow as tooling, and the commons.
## 8. Contributions

1. A mechanical diagnosis of FLF's compounding failure — search keys are private vocabulary — assembled from measured prior work, 1985–2016, and linked in two steps to FLF's stated limitation (§1).
2. A documented, dated case study from the author's own project: ~20 renamings, three retracted novelty claims, one foreclosed by a theorem; owners verified against primary text (§2).
3. A measured within-community retrieval check on FLF's eggs case, isolating specificity rather than neutrality (§5.1).
4. An exploratory pilot locating the value regime (the transparency gradient) and surfacing one three-decade-old owner missed by three manual audits (§2).
5. Two pre-registered negatives: curated synonym pairs could not test this tool class (memorized by construction on the vocabularies searched, §5.2), and definition fidelity rather than leakage was where generation first failed its contract (§5.3). A follow-up frozen set calibrated the fidelity gate, measured a checklist-guided fix that cleared it without retrieval gain, and returned a union-of-keys null (§5.4). These results motivate §4.1's component contract and key/representation split.
