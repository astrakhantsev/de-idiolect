# Glossary — "The vocabulary seam" (FLF entry)

This file accompanies the FLF competition entry *"The vocabulary seam: why cross-community knowledge doesn't compound, and a lazy way to bridge it"* (https://github.com/astrakhantsev/de-idiolect/blob/main/ENTRY.md) — "the entry" below, with all §-references pointing into it. It is the entry's own coin-time practice (§4.2) applied to the entry's own vocabulary: every project-local term below carries a plain-language definition and the field's nearest established term with its owner — the translation line the proposal says should be written when a term is minted. It doubles as an example of what the workflow surfaces for external readers of any project. Mappings are translations for readers, not novelty claims; per-claim grades live in the claims register (CLAIMS.md).

## A. Terms coined in this project

- **de-idiolect** (verb; **de-idiolection**, noun) — produce a description of a concept with the community's local vocabulary removed, and use it in place of the coined term. *Field's nearest:* no single established label found; descriptively, on-demand definition-mediated cross-community concept search — closest prior systems EDC, GenOM, SciCo-Radar (named and credited in §6). Coined here; a collision check found no prior usage in the searches on record. *Where:* §4.1.
- **the idiolect trap** — the failure the entry counters: a group coins private vocabulary, searches under it, finds nothing, and reads the confident null as unoccupied territory. *Field's nearest:* the vocabulary problem (Furnas, Landauer, Gomez & Dumais 1987) for the naming divergence; the jangle fallacy (Kelley 1927) for the renaming half; Blair & Maron 1985's precision-observable / recall-unobservable asymmetry for why the null feels trustworthy. *Where:* §1–§2.
- **the vocabulary seam** (title) — the mismatch surface between two communities' vocabularies, along which knowledge fails to travel or combine. *Field's nearest:* vocabulary mismatch (information retrieval); the community boundary that Star & Griesemer 1989's boundary objects are built to span. *Where:* title, §1.
- **confident null** — a prior-art or novelty search that returns nothing and *looks* thorough, because precision is visible and recall is not. *Field's nearest:* no established single term — a descriptive label; the mechanism is Blair & Maron 1985's precision/recall observability asymmetry. *Where:* §1, §2.
- **opaque coinage** — a coined term whose surface words do not reveal — or actively misdirect from — the concept it names (e.g. "the audit unit" routing to accounting). *Field's nearest:* semantically opaque / non-compositional term (lexicology). *Where:* §2.
- **transparency gradient** — the pilot's cross-case pattern: the definition layer's value tracks how semantically transparent the coinage is — opaque coinages benefit, transparent slogans don't. *Field's nearest:* semantic transparency / compositionality (lexicology), used here as a triage axis. Grade: hypothesis (CLAIMS C13). *Where:* §2.
- **misroute** — retrieval or model routing pulled to the field the term's *surface words* name rather than to the concept's owner. *Field's nearest:* vocabulary-mismatch retrieval failure (IR); early IR called a spurious hit a "false drop." *Where:* §2, §5.3–§5.4.
- **owner / owning literature** — the established field or work that already treats the concept under its own name. *Field's nearest:* prior art (patent vocabulary); antecedent literature. *Where:* throughout.
- **era-gated excerpts** — input documents restricted to those predating a cutoff event (a term's retraction, or its concept's discovery), so generated definitions cannot inherit later knowledge. *Field's nearest:* temporal split / time-based holdout; leakage control (ML evaluation). *Where:* §2, §5.3.
- **fidelity gate** — the frozen check a generated definition must pass before any candidate mapping proceeds to verification or publication (lossy definitions may still serve as retrieval keys, per the key/representation split): round-trip entailment against the source concept's commitments plus a ban on external names. *Field's nearest:* round-trip consistency / entailment-based verification (NLP). Calibration: CLAIMS C30. *Where:* §4.1, §5.3–§5.4.
- **key/representation split** — the design decision that retrieval keys may be cheap, lossy, and plural, while the faithful representation is required only at verification time. *Field's nearest:* blocking (cheap candidate generation) vs. matching (careful pairwise verification) in entity resolution / record linkage. *Where:* §4.1.
- **coin-time / claim-time / cite-time** — the three moments in one researcher's workflow where the inward interventions fire: when a term is minted, when a claim is stated, when a citation ships. *Field's nearest:* analogous to pre-commit hooks (software engineering) — deterministic checks at a workflow moment, as against standing instructions. *Where:* §4.2.
- **the commons (regenerable cache)** — the optional shared layer: a decentralized store of definitions and verified typed mappings with provenance — derived, versioned, forkable, never a canonical merge. *Field's nearest:* pay-as-you-go data integration / dataspaces (Franklin, Halevy & Maier 2005); emergent semantics (Aberer & Cudré-Mauroux 2004). *Where:* §4.1, §7.
- **peer reconciliation** — the evaluation regime where two live communities name overlapping concepts and neither is "the owner" — no established field to defer to. The live regime is the target; §5.5 tested a synthetic proxy with planted pairs. *Field's nearest:* ontology alignment between peer ontologies; terminology cross-walking. *Where:* §5.5, §7.
- **key (in §5.5)** — one synthetic evaluation unit: a pair of generated two-community corpora with 10 planted concept pairs; the *sealed key* is the held-out unit authored by an isolated model and fired once; because per-pair failures were never diagnosed into design changes, the key is not burned and remains valid for one future major version (§7). *Field's nearest:* held-out test set under a pre-registered train/test protocol. *Where:* §5.5.
- **Program A / Program B** — internal labels for the two research programs of the case study: A tried to build a source-dependence detector (how many effectively independent sources stand behind a verdict); B asked whether cross-judge LLM error correlation is reducible by elicitation. Their retracted coinages (`m*` / "the audit unit," "the operating requirement," "the signal, not the cut") are translated to their owners in §2's table. *Where:* §2.

## B. Established terms used, for readers outside the fields

- **jangle fallacy** — treating two things as different because they carry different names (Kelley 1927); its twin the **jingle fallacy** — treating two different things as the same because they share a name (Thorndike 1904, quoting Aikins).
- **construct identity** — whether two named constructs in the behavioral-science literature are in fact the same thing; a detection tool for it is Larsen & Bong 2016's CID.
- **boundary object** — an artifact that lets communities cooperate without agreeing on one classification (Star & Griesemer 1989).
- **controlled defining vocabulary** — a small fixed wordlist of ordinary words used to write all definitions; the LDOCE (Longman) tradition.
- **SKOS** (Simple Knowledge Organization System) — the W3C vocabulary for relations between concepts; the entry uses its names verbatim: `exactMatch`, `broadMatch`, `narrowMatch`, `relatedMatch` (W3C 2009).
- **automatic term recognition (ATR)** — the NLP subfield that detects domain-specific terms in corpora; the detection stage's field, and the author's former research field.
- **novel-sense detection** — detecting when an existing word is used in a new sense (the AXOLOTL'24 shared-task line).
- **keyness** — corpus-linguistics measure of how distinctive a word is for one corpus against a reference corpus; the prototype's detection heuristic.
- **onomasiological** — terminology-science direction of search: from the concept to its names (rather than from a name to its meanings).
- **reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)** — a standard IR method for combining several ranked lists into one; used (and returning a null) in §5.4.
- **value-of-information analysis** — decision theory's treatment of what information is worth gathering before acting (Raiffa & Schlaifer 1961 lineage, verified at secondary level per the entry); the entry's identified owner of "the operating requirement."
- **proper scoring rule** — a forecast score that is optimized by reporting true probabilities; the frame of Bröcker 2008, whose uniqueness theorem foreclosed Program B's exact theoretical claim — under the theorem's sufficiency condition and strictly proper scoring.
- **MeSH** — Medical Subject Headings, the NLM's curated biomedical thesaurus; the curated vocabulary of §5.2's negative.
