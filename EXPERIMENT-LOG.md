## E1 (§5.1) — A retrieval check on FLF's eggs case

Can the core move — using a definition rather than a term as the retrieval key — route correctly? A small prototype (~170 lines + a pluggable LLM backend; published ‹URL›) measures that link on FLF's eggs case, offline and deterministically (`bge-large-en-v1.5`). The check is **conditional on an oracle-supplied concept**: the three specialist concepts and their owners are hand-supplied, so it tests the definition-as-key move, not whether a lay question *discovers* the concepts. For three concepts surrounding a lay *"are eggs bad for me?"* question but unavailable to its vocabulary, the rank of the owning sub-field's best document among 15 is (lower is better):

| concept (owning sub-field) | naive question | raw term | **constrained definition** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| hyper-responder (lipidology) | 5 | 1 | **1** |
| apolipoprotein B particle number (cardiology biomarkers) | 4 | 1 | **1** |
| isocaloric substitution model (nutrition-epi methods) | 8 | 1 | **1** |

The naive question surfaces lay overviews and buries the owner at 4–8. The definition (term-free, prompt-constrained — the enforced wordlist is not implemented) brings the owner to rank 1, matching the raw term unavailable to the lay asker. The isolated effect is **specificity, not vocabulary-neutrality**: the ablation varied that axis and found no rank difference (E1a below; inconclusive). Testing neutrality's intended payoff — reaching a *second* community — requires the cross-community evaluation omitted here (entry §7). n = 3 concepts, 15 documents, one case, one model.

**The prototype's three limits, each visible in the code.** (1) **Detection is not wired into the measured path.** The keyness step runs and surfaces community-local terms, but the three evaluated concepts and their owning communities are hand-supplied; this is not an end-to-end run. (2) **The committed offline run uses author-written definitions** behind the real interface; in a live run (`--backend claude`), the model generated the constrained definitions and preserved the routing — all three live-generated definitions still pulled the owner to rank 1. The generator therefore produces definitions that route for these three concepts; reliability at scale is untested. (3) **Retrieval is to the concept's own owner community**, not concept-to-concept alignment between two specialist communities; the naive question is the baseline rather than a second community. No precision–recall evaluation exists; rank 1 is owner-rank (hit@1) evidence, not precision of the retrieved set. The design argument for precision is that the pipeline re-*specifies* rather than *broadens*, while the entry §4.1's pairwise verification stage is the designed precision guard — unmeasured in this run. A precision–recall curve on a real corpus with relevance judgments is owed. The one measured precedent for both metrics improving together in this setting is Larsen & Bong's construct-identity experiment (precision .68 vs .46, recall .58 vs .39 against full-text search) — a close analogy, not a measurement of this pipeline.

## E1a — The vocabulary ablation (run on our own open claim; inconclusive)

For the same three concepts, we generated constrained (plain words, no jargon) and free-text (natural expert prose) definitions, both forbidden the term itself, k = 3 samples on two generator models, and compared the owning literature's rank. Both arms reached rank 1 for every concept and sample under both models. The metric was saturated, so no difference was observed (which is not evidence of equivalence). On the only metric with headroom (separation margin), the free-text arm leads. A saved lexical screen shows that the free arm's margin co-occurs with soft term-restatement (near-headword forms missed by a whole-string leak check), while the constrained arm reaches rank 1 without them. A co-occurrence screen cannot establish matching-on-meaning, and an n = 3 rank tie is not non-inferiority. Three limits keep this from updating the open claim by more than a little: it tests jargon-avoidance *prompting*, not an enforced controlled-vocabulary word list; generation and retrieval use the same small corpus (circular by construction); and it does not measure the intended property of the constraint — cross-community neutrality — because the corpus has no cross-community pairs. The proposed funded evaluation, not a result claimed here, would use an enforced word list, disjoint corpora, true A↔B pairs, and a community-identity probe. *(Harness, frozen definitions, the lexical screen, and the adversarial review that produced this paragraph's caveats are in the materials.)*

## E2 (§5.2) — A cross-community cell on curated vocabulary (pre-registered; negative)

A pre-registered cell on a curated-vocabulary pair (MeSH) triggered its success rule at a threshold later shown to be at chance. It supports no retrieval claim; three adversarial reviews led us to retract claims briefly based on it.

A systematic search for a second cell found no valid test pair in the curated vocabularies searched, for a structural reason: **curated synonyms are memorized by construction**. Any pair prominent enough for a curated thesaurus is already reconciled in training corpora, so a frontier model bridges it from weights (measured on the vocabularies, models, and cutoffs searched; the mechanism argues it generalizes, but that is an argument). This narrows the candidate clean regimes to **post-cutoff concepts and private coinages** — the former unevaluated, the latter represented by exactly one clean exploratory cell (entry §2). *(Measured; pre-registration and review logs in the materials.)*

## E3 (§5.3) — An end-to-end cell on the project's own coinage (pre-registered; negative, with baseline)

A second pre-registered cell ran the generative path end-to-end on the entry §2's "operating requirement" (true owner: the value-of-information literature), using frozen pre-discovery excerpts.

**Primary result: both answer-blind constrained definitions failed a frozen round-trip fidelity check on the same feature (prospectivity)** before retrieval could be scored. Each collapsed "would an instrument this accurate be *worth building*" into "is this produced estimate worth using." This replicates the entry §2's definitional drift two-for-two in fresh generations, on a different axis, caught before retrieval by the gate. The supported conclusion is that **fidelity, not leakage, is where the generation stage first failed its contract**. Leak checks passed first-attempt in all arms, and E4 later showed that passing the gate produced no retrieval gain; fidelity is therefore a validity requirement, not a performance lever. This result produced the entry §4.1's component contract. Separately, outside the pre-registration, the retrospective detection demo missed its endpoint (the coinage ranked ~224th; raw keyness under-delivers) — two distinct failures, counted as two.

A **grounded direct-model baseline** — the same excerpts and 18-document corpus, given to a frontier model — ranked the true owner literature 1–2. Because the baseline had the corpus, its success does not separate parametric memory from in-context inference and cannot map the reconciled/unreconciled boundary. It establishes that, against an equal-information direct ask, the pipeline's mechanical form earned **no measured margin on this cell**. The remaining case concerns the entry §4.2's process properties (deterministic triggering, corpus-scale surfacing, isolation, provenance, durable mappings), with post-cutoff and private-pair seams as candidate regimes rather than demonstrated advantages. (Closest measured precedent for that boundary, pre-LLM and human-scale: in Larsen & Bong's follow-up studies, their detector outperformed all 36 human participants on one *uncommon*-construct task, while curated keyword search plus expert recall won the common-construct tasks; 314 of their 347 distinct construct categories — 90.5% — were classified as uncommon. An analogy across eras.)

Post-gate exploratory observations, in one list:

- The bare coinage reached an owner at rank 1 — by a near-tie margin on a flat ranking.
- The realistic in-project phrase misrouted to the fields named by its surface words (first owner rank 5) — the entry §2's misroute in embedding space.
- The restated question and two drifted definitions placed first owners at 2, 2, and 1.
- A one-run extractive-key probe (raw excerpt pool as query — drift-free but idiolect-laden) placed the first owner at rank 3, below two misroute documents — motivating the entry §4.1's warning that noisy keys are not free.

In this cell, retrieval reached the owner despite the drift, as did a plain restated question, motivating the entry §4.1's key/representation split. Drift tolerance beyond this cell is untested, and the entry §2 shows that drift *can* corrupt comparisons.

## E4 (§5.4) — Follow-up measurements on the failed stage (second frozen set)

A second frozen specification measured the gate, the candidate fix, and one design suggestion from E3 on the same cell (endpoints frozen before running; run records, spec hashes, and review-fold logs in the materials):

- **The gate itself** *(pre-registered)*. Four author-written faithful definitions all pass the frozen fidelity check; five definitions with seeded single defects all fail on exactly the planted item — false positives 0/4, detection 5/5, attribution 5/5. This is bounded to author-constructed materials and adjudication across four planted defect axes: a non-degeneracy check, not a population false-positive estimate. E3's negative rests on a gate shown to pass faithful definitions.
- **A cheap fix clears it** *(pre-registered)*. An isolated model call extracted a six-item checklist of the concept's commitments from the frozen excerpts alone (leak-clean; it recovered roughly four of five items in a human-written reference checklist — on this cell, extracting commitments appears easier than compressing them into a definition). Definitions regenerated with that checklist pass the frozen gate two-for-two, versus zero-for-two unguided (E3), including the item that failed both original configurations. This is consistent with the fix helping on this cell, but is not a causal claim — n = 1 per config, same machinery, no contemporaneous unguided replicate. One validity caveat remains: the frozen judge accepted prospectivity of the *threshold* ("fixed in advance, checkable up front"); under a stricter reading — prospectivity of the build decision itself — the guided definitions arguably still fail.
- **Fidelity bought no routing** *(measured (exploratory))*. The two gate-passing definitions place the first owner at ranks 2 and 3, no better than the drifted definitions they replace. Fidelity and retrieval do not move together on this corpus. This supports the entry §4.1's key/representation split and removes any implication that faithful definitions retrieve better.
- **Union-of-keys: null** *(pre-registered)*. Under a frozen reciprocal-rank-fusion rule, no union of keys weakly dominated the best single key — here the bare coinage, which held first-owner rank 1 and alone pulled a third owner into the top five — while every fusion promoted the corpus's misroute document. Combining keys also combines their misroute mass; the entry §4.1's multi-key proposal remains conjectural, now with a one-cell null.

The answer-aware corpus and single-cell limits of E3 still apply; none of these measurements reaches the cross-community target. (A fourth measurement from the same set — provenance-verification yield on the project's citations — appears where it applies, entry §4.2.)

## E5 (§5.5) — The peer-reconciliation evaluation (pre-registered; sealed held-out test; conservative-failure result)

We implemented and ran the synthetic core of the entry §7's continuation design: eight pre-registered pipeline versions on synthetic two-community corpora, with 10 planted pairs per key — no live-community or wild-pair claim. Different model families authored each side's coinages and used them in-register; definitions were generated blind to the other side; verification was bilateral, with each definition checked only against the other side's usage, never definition-against-definition; "no match despite surface similarity" was a first-class verdict; and each typed relation included a shared core and per-side residues.

The runs followed a train/test protocol with a sealed held-out key — procedurally sealed: an isolated model authored it, and the orchestrating agent never read its concept descriptions. Iterating on one key overfit as the protocol expected (dev-counterfactual 8/10 → fresh-key 4/10); after a pre-registered train pass, the sealed key was spent once. Because per-pair failures were never diagnosed into design changes, the key is not burned and remains valid for one future major version.

**The sealed test missed its pre-registered bar: 5/10 correct at the primary operating point, with the other two pre-registered points also at 5/10 (required ≥ 7).** Its held-out operating characteristics are the transferable result. Detection precision was 1.00 at every pre-registered operating point, measured on the later keys and all sampling draws. The only two false positives in eight scored runs occurred in one early iteration, were traced to a prompt-level decisiveness instruction, and did not recur after its reversion. Held-out coverage was 0.6: three pairs exhausted generation-stage artifact gates, and one failed quote validation. Detection made zero errors among the six detection-decided pairs, and all five hard relation assertions were typed correctly.

The recurring negative extends E3–E4: from v0.8 onward, the binding layer shifted from definition fidelity to excerpt evidence density and then generated-artifact gate survival. Earlier versions also revealed aggregation, retrieval-path, and relation-composition defects, each corrected toward frozen specifications under review. *(Measured; pre-registrations, five adversarial review rounds on the final two versions, per-version verdicts, and the sealed-key protocol log in the materials.)*
